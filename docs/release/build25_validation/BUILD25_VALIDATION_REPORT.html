1. Executive summary

Technical integrity

Passed, with one defect

No coin or item was created or lost at any of 111 checkpoints. Every economy save loaded back exactly. One script error in normal seed 1 (gossip) fails the workflow; cause reproduced, two-line fix ready, not applied.

Money circulation

Poor

By day 365 the chapel holds 77–83% of all coins. Its fund pays out only when a household lacks food, fuel, medicine or rent, which well-fed households never do, so the coins rarely return.

Long-term sustainability

Not demonstrated

Population roughly triples, but the number of jobs stays at about 45. By the end 67–77% of adults are out of work, median household cash is 4–11 coins, and households barely form. No one starved.

The simulation runs a full year without breaking the books. That is not the same as a working economy. The village survives because food stays plentiful and relief exists. Money drains into the chapel, work does not grow with the people, and buying power collapses. These patterns appear in every earlier year-long run too, so they come from long-standing design, not from the Expansion 20 merges. The Living Economy, switched on in one run, does not change this: households wanted to bid 12,065 times and could not afford to.

Recommendation: fix the gossip script error (category A) before freezing Build 25, then decide on the chapel's payout rule and on job and household growth (category B). Each needs your approval; none has been changed.

4. Population and employment

  • Growth comes from births and hungry newcomers. Normal seed 1 took in 170 people and lost 49; 146 of the newcomers left neighbouring settlements because of hunger. Seed 2: 124 in (99 for hunger), 70 out. Economy on: 138 in (110 for hunger), 25 out.
  • Jobs do not grow. The number of adults with a job stays between 40 and 63 all year in every run while adults rise from 60 to 140–199. Every earlier run shows the same, with 41 to 66 jobs at day 365.
  • Households barely form. 25 households at day 0 become 26–29 by day 365 while the population reaches 279–363. In normal seed 1, 22 households are crowded at the end and 55 single adults have no room to marry into.
  • No starvation in any Build 25 run. The pre-calibration soak's seed 1 did starve (81 deaths); its chapel was drained to 0.5% of coins, which shows the fund does pay out when food runs short.

5. Coins: conservation and circulation

Conservation (technical)

Coin leak 0, holder mismatch 0 and item leaks 0 at all 37 checkpoints of each run. Coin sources from outside (caravans and settlements) added 3,323, 3,600 and 3,488 coins. In the economy-on run the rental escrow ended at 0 with no open rentals, claims breaches were 0, and all 37 economy saves loaded back exactly (economy save 486–648 KB).

Circulation

How coins reach the chapel. On each weekly service day, villagers at or above a piety threshold give a share of their coins above their reserve. How coins leave it. Clergy wages (two low-paid people), food it buys for relief when a household asks, and alms. Alms go only to households short of their "necessity reserve": the coins needed for food, firewood, medicine and rent they lack, after counting what the pantry and members already hold (World.necessity, Chapel.alms). A household with a full pantry needs nothing by that measure, however few coins it has. With 10–25 days of food in store, almost no household qualifies, so the fund keeps growing. The code comment cites your rule: "offerings keep the priest, and what is left goes as alms to the poorest households." The implementation keeps what is left unless a household is short.

Not recorded in these runs: the chapel's own donation, alms and relief totals (kept only as world statistics, not in the samples), the full spread of household cash (only minimum, median, maximum and total), prices, and per-business profit.

6. Production, trade and household purchasing power

  • Food is plentiful. 10–25 days of food at day 365; meals missed because the household had no money: 5, 25 and 42; missed because none was for sale: 52, 4 and 7.
  • Firewood is short. 4,022–4,988 household-nights without firewood per run (earlier runs: 2,543–4,668). The consequence for health or warmth is not recorded.
  • Traders end broke. At day 365, 12 of the 15–18 traders (cooks, merchants, smiths, tanners, tavern keepers) hold no coins.
  • Trade outside continues. 182 caravans per run. Caravans brought in 4,809, 4,504 and 4,790 coins and took out 2,051, 1,086 and 1,498.
  • Manor work. The manor's jobs were taken 283, 382 and 289 times; it paid 6,790–7,794 coins in wages and collected 7,462–8,476 in tax over the year.

Not recorded: prices over time, business formation and failure in the village, wages per worker, profitability, and the value of stolen goods.

9. Findings by severity

A · Release blocker

Gossip script error (X20-TELL-GUARD)

Normal seed 1, between days 47 and 50: Out of bounds get index '171' in JudgmentStore._tell. A villager forgets a queued story mid-conversation (learning a refutation, or the memory cap) and the next read fails, cutting off the rest of that conversation. Reproduced on ad281dc. Becomes reachable more often since C2 made second-hand assault memories forgettable. Correction: two lines, skip a story already forgotten. Locally to day 52: no error, world unchanged, judgment state changed (the conversation finishes). Needs your approval and a re-validation decision.

B · Significant

The chapel absorbs most of the money

77–83% of all coins by day 365 in Build 25; 58–83% in earlier runs. Long-standing. Root cause: alms paid only against a necessity shortfall that food stocks erase. Smallest correction (for your decision): pay alms from what is left after the clergy's keep to the poorest households by cash held, as your rule reads, or set a ceiling on the fund above which the surplus goes as alms.

B · Significant

Work does not grow with the village

About 45 jobs all year while adults triple; 67–77% of adults idle at the end. Long-standing in every run. Hungry settlers keep arriving. Options (for your decision): let new workplaces open as people arrive, by existing business-creation rules; a cap on in-migration when there is no work; or treat idleness as accepted. A design choice, not a quick fix.

B · Significant

Buying power collapses

Median household cash falls from 63 to 4–11 coins, most traders end with nothing, and 12,065 market bids failed for lack of money. A consequence of the two findings above, not a separate defect.

B · Significant

Households barely form

25 households become 26–29 while the population nearly triples; adults stay in crowded homes. Long-standing. Needs a look at house building and marriage housing before any change.

C · Observation

Other observations

  • Firewood shortage: 4,000–5,000 household-nights a year without firewood; effect not recorded.
  • Villagers slain: 0–10 a year; the killer is not recorded.
  • Seed variation is large (population 279 against 359 on the same code).
  • Year-long determinism was not re-tested with a second identical run; the 30-day gate is identical three times.
  • Judgment, bandit, repair and rental systems behave as designed.

10. Recommended actions, ranked

  1. Approve the two-line gossip fix, then re-validate Build 25. Smallest choice: one normal seed-1 run to day 60, which covers the failing day, plus the 30-day gate. Any new Actions run needs your approval.
  2. Decide the chapel's payout rule. It is the main reason money stops circulating, and it affects every other economic number.
  3. Decide how work should grow with population. Planning belongs with Build 26's Living Economy work (LE-07, permanent employment).
  4. Review household formation and housing with the same evidence.
  5. Record the missing measures in future runs (chapel inflow and outflow, household cash spread, prices, business accounts), as analysis output only.
