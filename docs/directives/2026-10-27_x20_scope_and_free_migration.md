2. REG-X20-D1 — APPROVED: FREE MIGRATION

A villager may leave a settlement even when that villager is the only worker operating a business.

This applies to every profession.

THERE IS NO SPECIAL MIGRATION RESTRICTION FOR TRADERS.

A trader may move to another settlement because:

- A better market exists.
- Greater earning opportunities exist.
- Their current business is struggling.
- Food or other necessities are unavailable.
- Living conditions are unfavorable.
- Their individual circumstances make relocation attractive.

These are examples, not an exhaustive list.

The same general principle applies to blacksmiths, farmers, woodcutters, miners, merchants, and other professions.

A business being important to a settlement is not sufficient justification to prevent its owner or only worker from leaving.

If the departure creates an economic shortage, that shortage is a legitimate consequence for the settlement to address through normal simulation behavior.

Do not artificially retain an NPC to protect the village economy.

3. MIGRATION MUST REMAIN AN INDIVIDUAL DECISION

Migration is not mandatory merely because another market offers better prices.

NPCs should evaluate opportunities according to the existing approved decision-making framework, including their personal circumstances, economic interests, needs, relationships, and preferences.

Two traders in the same settlement may reasonably make different decisions.

Do not replace individual autonomy with a hard-coded migration requirement.

Preserve legitimate ownership, property, employment, and contractual consequences when someone relocates.

Handle the resulting business state under the established rules.

Do not invent new restrictions to prevent a departure.

4. SIMULATION REQUIREMENTS

The economy must be allowed to experience the consequences of voluntary migration.

For example:

If the only trader leaves a struggling village for a more profitable settlement, the original settlement may temporarily lose access to that trader's services.

Other villagers may recognize the opportunity and establish a replacement business.

Existing merchants may expand.

Trade relationships may change.

Prices may respond to actual supply and demand.

These outcomes must emerge through the approved economic systems, not through forced population retention or artificially generated resources.

Include migration, abandoned businesses, and their economic consequences in the flight-recorder diagnostics and LE-06 simulation reporting.
