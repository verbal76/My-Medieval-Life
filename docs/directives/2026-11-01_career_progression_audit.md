MY MEDIEVAL LIFE — EXPANSION 20

CAREER PROGRESSION, NPC AMBITION & ECONOMIC ADVANCEMENT AUDIT

OWNER DIRECTIVE

Claude, I want an additional investigation incorporated into our ongoing Economic Maturation Audit.

My Medieval Life is intended to simulate a society where people develop skills, pursue opportunities, advance in their professions, and contribute to the growth of the settlement.

Villagers should not remain permanently trapped in their starting occupations or skill levels.

As they work, they should gain experience, improve their abilities, qualify for more advanced work, and potentially move into better positions.

They should also have individual ambitions. A villager who wants to become a master craftsman should be capable of pursuing that goal through the systems already established in the game.

Career advancement should arise naturally from the villager's abilities, personality, circumstances, opportunities, and decisions.

This directive is an audit first, not authorization to redesign the career system.

1. DO NOT INTERRUPT EXISTING WORK

The existing Expansion 20 economic maturation investigation remains active.

Currently running:

- The 30-day seed 1 instrumentation-neutrality check.
- The X20-TELL-GUARD gossip reproduction investigation.

Allow both to continue uninterrupted.

Do not cancel, restart, duplicate, or interfere with either process.

Do not change the simulation code or instrumentation being validated.

Perform the initial career-progression investigation through source inspection and existing simulation evidence.

Incorporate the findings into the current economic maturation report rather than launching a separate, redundant simulation campaign.

2. DETERMINE WHAT IS ALREADY IMPLEMENTED

Inspect the actual code and existing requirements for:

1. Occupational experience accumulation.
2. Skill acquisition and improvement through work.
3. Apprenticeships and training.
4. Journeyman or intermediate professional development.
5. Master-level or advanced professional qualifications.
6. Career aspirations and NPC ambition.
7. NPC recognition of career opportunities.
8. Decisions to seek advancement or better employment.
9. Promotions within an existing workplace.
10. Career changes and retraining.
11. Wage or compensation progression.
12. Advanced production capabilities unlocked through skill.
13. Independent craftsmanship and self-employment.
14. Establishment of workshops, shops, and businesses.
15. Hiring and training apprentices or employees.
16. Replacement of retiring or deceased skilled workers.
17. Economic mobility between household wealth levels.
18. Constraints that prevent advancement.

For each mechanism, classify it as:

- Fully implemented and functioning.
- Implemented but not verified.
- Implemented but blocked or malfunctioning.
- Partially implemented.
- Not implemented.

Provide exact source files, relevant functions, and supporting evidence.

Do not assume that increasing a skill statistic means the villager can actually advance professionally.

3. DISTINGUISH SKILL DEVELOPMENT FROM CAREER ADVANCEMENT

These are separate systems that must interact correctly.

Skill development: A villager becomes more capable through practice, training, and experience.

Career advancement: The villager uses those improved capabilities to obtain a better professional position, undertake more advanced work, earn better compensation, or pursue independence.

Economic expansion: Advanced workers create additional productive capacity, establish businesses, train others, and generate new employment opportunities.

Determine whether all three stages exist and are connected.

For example, a blacksmith may begin as an apprentice, become a journeyman, and eventually qualify as a master.

A qualified master might continue working for an employer, establish an independent workshop, or train apprentices.

This example illustrates the intended relationship. Do not assume these exact titles, thresholds, or mechanics exist unless confirmed in the code and approved requirements.

Do not invent new professional ladders during this audit.

4. NPC AMBITION AND INDIVIDUAL DECISION-MAKING

Investigate whether NPCs actually want to advance.

Determine whether the game models:

- Personal career goals.
- Ambition and motivation.
- Satisfaction or dissatisfaction with current work.
- Recognition of improved qualifications.
- Desire for better compensation.
- Desire for independence or business ownership.
- Willingness to change employers.
- Willingness to undertake training.
- Recognition of available opportunities.
- Decisions influenced by household financial needs.
- Decisions influenced by personality, relationships, reputation, and circumstances.

Not every villager should have identical ambitions.

Some may be content remaining in their current position. Others may actively pursue advancement.

Do not impose automatic promotion or guaranteed success.

We want believable individuals making decisions, not a scripted conveyor belt that promotes everyone after a fixed number of days.

Determine whether NPC aspirations are actually connected to employment and career decisions or whether those systems operate independently.

5. INVESTIGATE THE SKILLED-EMPLOYMENT BOTTLENECK

The economic maturation audit reported:

- Skilled-trade headcounts remaining near their starting levels.
- Total employment remaining around 51–63 jobs.
- Unemployment reaching approximately 94–141 adults in the audited runs.

Investigate why.

Determine whether:

- Workers are gaining the skills needed for advanced employment.
- Qualified workers exist but cannot advance.
- Advanced positions are unavailable.
- Employers fail to recognize qualified candidates.
- Hiring systems are not creating new positions.
- Businesses are unable to expand.
- Skilled villagers cannot establish independent businesses.
- NPCs lack the decision-making logic to pursue advancement.
- Economic or resource constraints prevent expansion.
- Existing job limits are fixed or incorrectly enforced.
- Population growth is outpacing available productive employment.
- The simulation harness fails to exercise relevant progression systems.

Distinguish genuine defects from legitimate economic constraints.

Do not fix unemployment by artificially creating jobs.

We want jobs to emerge from actual production, trade, business development, and economic demand.

6. FOLLOW INDIVIDUAL VILLAGERS OVER TIME

Use existing records where possible to trace representative NPC careers.

For each available example, report:

- Starting occupation.
- Starting skill level.
- Starting economic condition.
- Experience gained.
- Skill improvements.
- Career aspirations.
- New qualifications achieved.
- Opportunities recognized.
- Promotions or job changes attempted.
- Promotions or job changes completed.
- Compensation changes.
- Business or self-employment attempts.
- Reasons advancement succeeded or failed.
- Final occupation and economic condition.

Prefer longitudinal records from the same individuals.

Do not compare unrelated villagers at different dates and present that as career progression.

If the necessary records are unavailable, identify the minimum observation-only instrumentation required.

Do not alter NPC decisions to generate better-looking results.

7. CONNECT CAREER PROGRESSION TO ECONOMIC MATURATION

The economic evaluation must determine whether career advancement contributes to the settlement's development.

Measure or identify how to measure:

- Apprentices entering trades.
- Apprentices qualifying for advanced work.
- Skilled workers advancing.
- Qualified workers unable to advance.
- Workers changing professions.
- Workers becoming independent producers.
- New workshops and shops established.
- Businesses hiring additional employees.
- Masters training new workers.
- New jobs created by expanding businesses.
- Income changes associated with advancement.
- Changes in production capacity.
- Changes in available goods and services.
- Changes in marketplace participation.

Evaluate progression over days 1, 30, 90, 180, 270, and 365, using the existing economic maturation framework.

Longer simulations should only be considered if the evidence indicates that career advancement naturally requires more time.

Do not assume that every villager should become a master within one year.

8. PRESERVE EXISTING DESIGN

Before proposing changes, inspect the established requirements and previous owner decisions concerning:

- NPC aspirations.
- Skill development.
- Professions and employment.
- Apprenticeships.
- Business ownership.
- Trade and commerce.
- Negotiated employment and procurement contracts.
- Independent economic activity.
- Population and household development.

Respect existing design decisions.

Do not invent arbitrary promotion thresholds, wage multipliers, skill caps, job quotas, or guaranteed business openings.

If a progression mechanism is missing, describe the missing functionality and propose how it could connect to the existing systems.

Do not implement new gameplay mechanics without owner authorization.

Confirmed bugs may be documented for prioritized correction, but this particular assignment authorizes investigation and recommendations only.

FINAL OWNER DIRECTIVE

My Medieval Life is supposed to be a living medieval society.

A villager who begins as an inexperienced worker should be capable of learning a profession, developing expertise, pursuing advancement, and potentially becoming a respected master or independent business owner.

That progression should arise from the villager's own motivations and circumstances, not from an artificial guarantee of success.

As individuals develop, their advancement should contribute to the growth of businesses, production, employment, and commerce throughout the settlement.

I want to know whether we actually built that complete progression system, whether NPCs are using it, and what is preventing it from functioning if they are not.

Audit what exists first. Identify the broken or missing connections. Show me the evidence before proposing changes.
