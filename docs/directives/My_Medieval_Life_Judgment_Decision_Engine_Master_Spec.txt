0. Read this first: non-negotiable intent
      Every character can form a subjective opinion of every other character; the player is not special.
      An action has an event-level GOOD or BAD baseline and severity, but each observer applies a personal signed
       relevance/interpretation percentage.
      The observer stores nonnegative GOOD and BAD totals separately and computes NET = GOOD − BAD. NET can
       be fractional (e.g., −6.59).
      The net judgment is a graded likeability/disposition influence. It is NOT the decision, NOT a universal
       reputation, NOT an automatic romance or commerce threshold, and NOT an instruction to obey.
      Each actual decision considers context-dependent factors such as need, children, food, money, resources,
       alternatives, ambitions, personality, danger, relationship goals, and judgment.
      Negative judgment can be overridden by necessity; positive judgment cannot make impossible or unwanted
       actions happen.
      The entire design must eventually integrate across the simulation, incrementally, after a stable Build 16
       equilibrium baseline is captured.

Canonical example: John judges Verbal at −6.59, but needs 50 iron and Verbal is the only supplier. John can still
buy iron because scarcity and family needs outweigh dislike. A second supplier may change the outcome without
changing John’s opinion.

1.1 In scope
Social, economic, survival, household, romantic, political, criminal, employment, resource, cooperative,
competitive, and interpersonal decisions; observed, reported, and inferred events; persistence; deterministic
replay; simulation analytics.

1.2 Out of scope for initial implementation

No LLM calls, cloud AI, network dependency, scripted individual opinions, wholesale NPC scheduler replacement,
universal hard-coded moral outcomes, guaranteed political/romantic behavior, or mandatory real-time all-pairs
relationship calculations.

1.3 Invariants
1.   World truth is separate from belief; belief is separate from moral judgment; judgment is separate from
     behavior.
2.   Event base polarity and severity do not mutate when an observer disagrees.
3.   Per-observer GOOD/BAD contributions never become negative; interpretation may reverse which ledger
     receives the magnitude.
4.   A single belief about an event has one active contribution per observer–actor–event identity; gossip does not
     add duplicates.
5.   Actions must be feasible before utility scoring can select them.
6.   All relevant weights, thresholds, policies, and feature flags are data driven and inspectable.
7.   Social systems must preserve their distinct concepts: familiarity, friendship, trust, respect, attraction,
     obligation, and moral judgment.
8.   No action should produce an unbounded flood of morally significant events merely through repetitive
     animation or passive ticks.

4. Subjective interpretation math
Let severity S ≥ 0, interpretation P ∈ [−1,1], and effective confidence C ∈ [0,1]. Define signed impact I = S × P × C.
This is a proposed initial formulation, not a settled truth model. Confidence may instead be used only for belief
gating; choose through tests and owner approval.

 Base event                                         I≥0                                        I<0

 GOOD                                               GOOD += I                                  BAD += abs(I)

                             HOT ATTIC GAMES • INTERNAL DESIGN HANDOFF • POST–BUILD 16
 BAD                                            BAD += I                       GOOD += abs(I)



Example: Bob is robbed of materials, S=1 BAD. Bob P=+1.00 → 1 BAD. Bob’s cousin P=+0.55 → 0.55 BAD. Rival John
P=−0.65 → 0.65 GOOD. Unaware person has no belief/contribution, not an automatic zero-valued judgment
record.

4.1 Interpreting percentages
P is signed subjective alignment with the event’s baseline, not “how much the NPC witnessed,” not probability of
knowing, and not a negative point. Direct impact, social proximity, personal values, economic advantage, belief
confidence, and personality can all influence it. Opposing forces can cancel or reverse P.

4.2 Proposed interpretable calculation
relevance = bounded_blend(personal_impact, social_impact,
              economic_impact, value_alignment,
              relationship_context, personality_context)
interpretation = clamp(relevance, -1.0, +1.0)
impact = severity * interpretation * confidence_policy(belief)
post_to_good_or_bad(impact, baseline_polarity)
The precise blend function is intentionally not fixed. A weighted average may suppress strong direct victim
reactions, while unbounded addition exaggerates correlated influences. Candidate approaches: hierarchical
priority for direct harm; bounded weighted sum; signed nonlinear aggregation. Compare them against a golden-
case fixture suite. Expose each component in diagnostics.

4.3 Guard against circular feedback
Prior dislike can bias interpretation of ambiguous behavior, but must not automatically convert every ordinary
action into escalating hostility. Cap relationship-bias effects, distinguish known harm from rumor, and avoid
double applying existing friendship, family bond, and loyalty to the same influence.


5. Ledger mechanics, examples, and ambiguity
Each observer→subject ledger contains total GOOD, total BAD, NET, active contribution count, belief confidence
summaries, familiarity/observation coverage, and a bounded history index. The totals remain separate even if
most ordinary decisions consume NET.

 Observer                    GOOD                          BAD         NET                      Meaning

 A                           0                             0           0                        Unfamiliar/indifferent
 B                           50                            50          0                        Extensive mixed
                                                                                                history
 John                        20                            26.59       −6.59                    Mildly unfavorable
 Friend                      32                            15          +17                      Favorable
 Close ally                  40                            15          +25                      Strongly favorable



100 actions with 50 baseline GOOD and 50 baseline BAD do not necessarily produce equal observer contributions.
Most actions may be unknown, irrelevant, weakly weighted, or reversed. Therefore an observer can reasonably
end at +2.65, −6.59, +17, or −30 from the same world history.

5.1 Update and correction
key = (observer_id, judged_actor_id, source_event_id)

old = active_contribution.get(key, ZERO)
new = evaluate_current_belief(event, observer, actor)
ledger.good += new.good - old.good
ledger.bad += new.bad - old.bad
active_contribution[key] = new

Store and update contributions idempotently. Retractions subtract prior credited points from the ledger, but
ledger totals themselves must not go negative. Apply fixed-point arithmetic (e.g., integer milli-points) or a
carefully specified rounding policy for deterministic replay and save compatibility.

5.2 Long-term calibration
Scores can inflate as event count grows. Evaluate event spam prevention, severity caps, diminishing marginal
impact for repeated identical behaviors, memory/forgiveness policies, and optional recency effects. Never add
automatic decay or universal caps without explicit owner decision. Preserve lifetime historical totals if a separate
effective/recent judgment is introduced.


6. Belief, knowledge, evidence, and rumor
Four separate records are recommended: factual event, communicated claim, observer belief, and observer
contribution. A villager who heard an accusation can judge the accused even if the world truth differs. On
correction, reverse/recompute the old contribution; do not post the original accusation again as an independent
duplicate moral event.

 Knowledge source                                           Possible effects

 Direct participant                                         High access to consequences, but motive may remain
                                                            unknown
 Eyewitness                                                 Observed action, limited knowledge of intent
 Trusted testimony                                          Confidence depends on messenger credibility
 Rumor                                                      Uncertain actor, motive, or facts; may propagate
 Fabricated claim                                           False belief can influence decisions until corrected
 Evidence discovery                                         Updates actor attribution or context; triggers recomputation



Open questions: rumor travel mechanics, credibility scoring, whether believing a false allegation damages the
accuser’s judgment upon discovery, and whether observers remember that they were deceived. Proposed staged
rollout: firsthand and direct participant events first; rumor and evidence later, with schema support from day
one.


7. Universal decision architecture
The decision engine must not use a universal “judgment threshold → action” table. It should assess feasible
alternatives using decision-specific considerations. A judgment of −6.59 supplies a small negative preference
signal; needs and opportunities may dominate.

9.   Generate available actions based on NPC needs, goals, environment, opportunities, and scheduled obligations.
10. Check hard feasibility: inventory, money/credit/barter, mobility, access, required relationships, laws, consent,
     time, and safety.
11. For each feasible action, assemble a typed context and select a DecisionProfile.
12. Evaluate factors independently, normalizing each to a documented common scale.
13. Apply profile weights, nonlinear conditions, personality/values, and bounded judgment influence.

14. Select an action by configured policy: best score or seeded bounded exploration; selection probability must
     not be confused with utility score.
15. Execute via existing systems and record actual consequences, not hypothetical intentions.
16. Publish significant outcome events and update affected beliefs/ledgers.

score(action, context, npc) =
  weighted_factors(action, context, npc)
 + interaction_terms(action, context, npc)
 + judgment_influence(npc, participants, action_type)
 - contextual_risks(action, context, npc)


eligible(action) = all(hard_constraints_pass)

7.4 Opposing motivations
NPCs may behave inconsistently across situations without violating internal logic. A merchant can despise a
customer but sell to feed a family. A spouse can love a partner but refuse to cover up a murder. A candidate can
be personally disliked but supported for a specific village policy. Do not automatically update judgment simply
because an NPC chooses to cooperate.

9. Relationships, romance, and politics: preserve distinctions
Existing friendship, familiarity, trust, romance, gifts, and courtship pacing remain distinct. Positive judgment can
increase willingness to engage, but cannot create attraction or consent. Existing multi-day contact requirements



                      HOT ATTIC GAMES • INTERNAL DESIGN HANDOFF • POST–BUILD 16
must not be bypassed by a single high-scoring event. Political preferences may conflict with personal liking;
support must evaluate candidate goals and village interests as well as judgment.


10. Personality, values, needs, and goals
NPCs should differ in factor weights and thresholds, not just in cosmetic dialogue. Trait profiles must be bounded,
interpretable, and consistent. A proud NPC may refuse an unpleasant supplier when alternatives exist; a
pragmatic NPC may prioritize essential supplies; a generous NPC may share excess food; a fearful NPC may refuse
risky assistance despite affection.

Recommended hierarchy: immutable/slow traits → medium-term priorities and social ties → current
physiological/economic needs → decision-specific opportunities → selection. Ensure needs are continuously
updated by existing simulation mechanics and are not fabricated for scoring convenience.


11. Time, consequences, and feedback loops
Every selected action can change resources, schedules, social states, and future options. Only meaningful
consequences generate judgment events. Example: John buys iron despite dislike → iron and money change hands
→ if Verbal fulfills the agreement fairly, a small positive event may be perceived → John’s NET becomes slightly
less negative → later trade becomes somewhat easier. If Verbal cheats him, the opposite occurs.

Avoid unstable positive feedback: disliking someone must not automatically generate a BAD event on every
avoidance tick; a single refusal should not recursively trigger infinite retaliation. Add causal chains, event
deduplication, cooldowns, and safeguards for action→event→decision loops.

12.2 Pseudocode: decision processing
decide(npc, situation):
    ctx = context_builder.build(npc, situation)
    candidates = action_provider.enumerate(ctx)
    feasible = [a for a in candidates if constraints_pass(a, ctx)]
    if feasible.is_empty(): return fallback_idle_or_replan(ctx)
    scores = [utility.evaluate(a, ctx, judgment_store) for a in feasible]
    chosen = seeded_selection(scores, ctx.seed_stream)
    diagnostics.record(ctx, scores, chosen)
    return executor.execute(chosen)


12.3 Data versioning and saves
Persist event IDs, active beliefs, contribution snapshots, GOOD/BAD ledgers, deterministic RNG state/streams, and
schema version. Implement migrations and corruption checks. Keep separate world-truth and subjective belief
stores. Avoid expensive reconstruction from the entire world event log on every load; provide an offline ledger-
rebuild validator for QA.


13. Performance, scale, and deterministic replay
      Event-driven updates, not every-NPC-to-every-NPC scoring each frame.
      Sparse observer→subject ledgers, allocated when relevant.
      Candidate actions limited to plausible, nearby, socially connected, or goal-relevant choices.
      Cache net judgment; invalidate on contribution changes.
      Use budgeted processing for rumor propagation and distant NPCs.
      Maintain deterministic ordering for simultaneous events and actor/observer iteration.
      Use fixed-point arithmetic or explicitly deterministic rounding.
      Separate simulation ticks from frame-rate-dependent animation.
      Benchmark population sizes and save/load duration against Build 16 baseline before setting hard budgets.

Recommended metrics: judgments processed per simulated day, active ledgers, average event fanout, decision
evaluations per day, peak frame time, simulation days per wall-clock minute, memory footprint, save size, and
deterministic hash comparison.

20. Acceptance checklist
   Every NPC can judge every other NPC using the same architecture.
   Different observers can assign opposite signs to one event.
   Fractional weighted contributions and net scores are mathematically correct.
   GOOD/BAD histories are preserved separately and corrections are idempotent.
   Net judgment is only one contextual decision factor.
   NPCs may trade or cooperate despite negative judgment when circumstances justify it.
