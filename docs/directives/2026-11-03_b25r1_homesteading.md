MY MEDIEVAL LIFE — BUILD 25 REVISION 1

MASTER OWNER DIRECTIVE: INDEPENDENT HOMESTEADING & GENERATIONAL RURAL SETTLEMENT

STATUS: APPROVED FOR IMPLEMENTATION

Scope classification: Completion and extension of the existing living-world population, regional geography, construction, land ownership, household, economy, and survival systems.

Integrate this directive into the ongoing Build 25 Revision 1 work. Preserve the existing Build 25 private draft as a rollback point.

Do not move this work into Build 26 or create an unrelated simulation framework.

---

1. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

My Medieval Life simulates people living their lives in a persistent medieval world.

Not everyone will want to live inside a village or town.

Some people will eventually choose to establish independent households in the countryside, particularly as children grow into adults and begin their own lives.

This behavior must emerge from the existing population and simulation.

Fundamental rules:

- Homesteaders are existing NPCs with real identities and histories.
- Homesteading is uncommon, gradual, and generational.
- Established settlements must remain viable communities.
- Independent land is not automatically free of danger or responsibility.
- Homesteaders must obtain materials and construct their homes through legitimate gameplay.
- The player must have the same opportunity to establish an independent homestead.
- All residents remain part of the persistent living-world simulation.

Do not create decorative wilderness households or fake residents.

---

2. FREE WILDERNESS LAND

Land outside the recognized jurisdiction, ownership, or influence of established settlements should generally be available for independent occupation.

Land eligibility

Use existing systems for:

- Settlement influence and boundaries
- Property ownership
- Terrain classification
- Roads and trails
- Water and resources
- Building placement
- Existing protected or restricted locations

A homesteader may occupy eligible, unclaimed wilderness land without purchasing it from a settlement.

However, land is not eligible when it is:

- Already owned or occupied
- Inside an existing settlement's protected or controlled area without appropriate rights
- Reserved for roads or other public infrastructure
- Impassable or unsuitable for the intended structure
- Otherwise restricted by established world rules

Do not invent an entirely separate land ownership system if the existing property system can be extended.

Land claims

A valid homestead claim must have:

- A persistent owner or household
- Actual world coordinates
- Defined occupied land or property boundaries
- A record that prevents overlapping claims
- Save/load persistence

A homestead claim does not automatically create a village, town, government, or new tax jurisdiction.

---

3. WHO MAY BECOME A HOMESTEADER?

A small minority of existing NPCs may choose independent rural life.

Primary candidates

Young adults who have reached the existing age of independent household formation.

As children mature, their life choices may include:

1. Remaining in their family household.
2. Establishing a new household within their home settlement.
3. Moving to another established settlement.
4. Occasionally establishing an independent wilderness homestead.

Homesteading must remain the uncommon option.

Secondary candidates

Established adults may occasionally relocate when their circumstances provide a compelling reason.

Examples include:

- Access to valuable resources
- Agricultural opportunity
- Personal ambition
- Desire for independence
- Suitable land near an existing trade route
- Family or occupational circumstances

Existing adults should not routinely abandon established homes merely because wilderness land is available.

No artificial population creation

Do not spawn new people to populate wilderness homesteads.

Use actual residents and existing demographic processes.

Retain their identities, relationships, occupations, skills, possessions, and histories.

---

4. STRICT POPULATION PROTECTION

Owner requirement: Villages must not gradually empty because everyone wants to avoid taxes.

Independent homesteading is intended to produce a small, sustainable rural population alongside established settlements.

Departure safeguards

- Only a small minority of eligible young adults should choose homesteading.
- Existing adult relocation must be substantially less common.
- No fixed annual percentage of the entire village population may depart.
- No guaranteed homesteader quota.
- Limit departures from settlements experiencing sustained population decline.
- Account for the number of working-age residents and viable households.
- Avoid repeated departures from the same household without a legitimate reason.
- Do not create replacement residents to compensate for departures.
- Preserve normal births, deaths, aging, marriage, inheritance, and migration.

Initial tuning

Use approximately 1–3% of eligible young adults at independent household formation as a provisional starting range, not an annual percentage of the whole population.

This is a tuning hypothesis, not a guaranteed departure rate.

Actual departures must depend on eligibility, circumstances, settlement health, available land, and individual motivation.

Calibrate the final rate through demographic simulations.

Taxes

Taxes may be one minor consideration in an individual's broader economic circumstances, but must never become a dominant or automatic reason for widespread migration.

Do not implement a simplistic rule where high taxes cause large numbers of villagers to leave.

Do not make independent living universally more profitable than settlement life.

A wilderness homestead has meaningful costs and risks that must be represented.

---

5. HOMESTEAD LOCATION SELECTION

NPCs must evaluate actual world geography rather than choosing arbitrary coordinates.

Consider:

- Water availability
- Nearby timber
- Stone and ore resources
- Fertile or workable land
- Existing roads and trails
- Distance from settlements and markets
- Terrain suitability
- Wildlife danger
- Bandit activity
- Availability of construction materials
- Existing claims and restrictions

A farmer might favor fertile land and water.

A miner might favor accessible ore deposits.

A woodworker might favor timber.

These preferences should emerge from existing occupations, skills, ambitions, and practical needs.

Do not invent new occupations solely to support homesteading.

---

6. PHYSICAL RELOCATION AND CONSTRUCTION

Homesteading must occur through actual world actions.

Required sequence

1. An eligible NPC decides to pursue independent settlement.
2. The NPC identifies an eligible location.
3. The NPC evaluates available money, materials, tools, and skills.
4. The NPC physically travels to the location.
5. The NPC establishes a valid land claim.
6. The NPC gathers or purchases construction materials.
7. The NPC constructs an initial shelter using the existing building system.
8. The NPC establishes basic storage and sleeping arrangements.
9. The NPC begins maintaining an independent livelihood.

Do not instantly generate a completed house with free furniture, food, or supplies.

Construction integrity

Use existing systems for:

- Building materials
- Construction work
- Skill progression
- Labor and hiring
- Tools
- Inventory
- Resource consumption
- Building persistence

A person lacking the skills to complete construction may seek assistance through the existing labor and trade systems.

Construction should consume real resources.

Failed attempts

An NPC may fail to establish a viable homestead.

Possible outcomes include:

- Returning to the original settlement
- Seeking work elsewhere
- Relocating to another viable site
- Running out of supplies
- Injury or death through existing gameplay systems

Failure must not automatically erase the NPC or their possessions.

---

7. INDEPENDENT DAILY LIFE

Once established, homesteaders should continue normal simulated lives.

They must manage applicable needs through the existing systems:

- Food
- Water
- Shelter
- Fuel
- Health
- Work
- Resource gathering
- Construction and maintenance
- Inventory
- Economic activity
- Social relationships

They may farm, mine, gather timber, hunt, craft, or undertake other work supported by their existing skills and world opportunities.

Do not create special unlimited resource supplies for isolated households.

Homesteaders remain subject to the same resource depletion, sustainability, and conservation rules as other residents.

---

8. CONNECTION TO THE REGIONAL ECONOMY

Independent living does not mean complete isolation.

Homesteaders may:

- Travel to settlements to purchase supplies.
- Sell goods they produce.
- Negotiate ordinary trade transactions.
- Seek employment or hire workers.
- Visit family and acquaintances.
- Maintain friendships and relationships.
- Participate in other supported social and economic activities.

Use the existing living economy and transaction systems.

No artificial money or goods may be created merely to make a homestead economically viable.

Identity and residence

Preserve the distinction between:

- An NPC's original settlement and personal history
- Their current residence
- Their current physical location
- Their applicable legal or civic affiliation

Do not automatically erase prior citizenship or civic relationships when residence changes. Follow existing jurisdiction rules where defined.

---

9. BANDITS, WILDLIFE AND WILDERNESS DANGER

Owner requirement: Independent homesteading carries genuine danger.

Homesteaders must remain vulnerable to bandits and hostile animals.

Bandits

Bandits may discover or approach isolated homesteads through normal world behavior.

Applicable interactions may include:

- Threats
- Robbery
- Theft
- Combat
- Injury
- Death
- Flight and pursuit

Bandits must use the existing AI, combat, mortality, and decision-making systems.

Do not automatically target every homestead or create guaranteed attacks.

Wildlife

Hostile wildlife may threaten people living in suitable wilderness habitats.

Animal behavior must follow existing ecological and combat rules.

Do not spawn hostile animals directly onto homesteaders merely to create danger.

Defensive behavior

Homesteaders may respond according to their existing abilities:

- Defend themselves
- Flee
- Seek shelter
- Seek assistance
- Cooperate with nearby residents

Do not grant homesteaders invulnerability or artificial combat bonuses.

Risk and reward

Living near valuable resources may provide economic advantages, but isolation increases exposure to danger and makes trade and assistance less convenient.

These tradeoffs should arise naturally from the simulation.

---

10. FAMILIES, CHILDREN AND GENERATIONS

Homesteads must support normal household and family continuity.

Existing marriage, children, aging, mortality, and inheritance systems remain applicable.

Family development

A homesteader may:

- Marry
- Form or maintain a household
- Have children
- Raise children
- Develop the property
- Pass property to heirs through existing inheritance rules

Children born at a homestead must be actual members of the persistent population.

They must not be decorative NPCs.

Generational settlement

As children grow into adulthood, they may choose ordinary life paths, including remaining at the homestead, moving to a settlement, or occasionally establishing their own household elsewhere.

Do not automatically create a new homestead for every adult child.

Population balance

Homesteading must remain a minority behavior across generations.

Monitor both established settlement populations and independent rural households to prevent uncontrolled expansion or village depopulation.

---

11. HOMESTEAD DEVELOPMENT

Successful homesteaders may gradually improve their property.

Use existing construction and resource systems for applicable additions such as:

- Larger homes
- Storage buildings
- Gardens
- Farmland
- Workshops
- Livestock enclosures
- Other supported structures

Do not instantly grant improvements.

Every development must obey the applicable resource, construction, and ownership rules.

Nearby households

Multiple independent households may eventually settle in the same general area.

Their structures and residents must remain persistent.

Do not automatically transform a cluster of homes into a formal village.

Formal settlement creation, governance, taxation, and civic organization require their own established rules or separately approved design.

---

12. PLAYER HOMESTEADING

The player must be able to use the same eligible wilderness land.

Player capabilities

- Locate suitable unclaimed land.
- Establish a valid claim.
- Gather or purchase materials.
- Build a shelter.
- Expand the property through existing construction systems.
- Store possessions.
- Live and work outside established settlements.
- Trade with settlements.
- Experience normal wilderness risks.

The player must not receive unlimited free materials or special exemptions.

Do not require the player to establish a formal village simply to build an independent home.

Ensure that normal player interactions, construction, inventory, and save/load work outside settlement boundaries.

This is especially important given the existing Build 25 wilderness interaction defects.

---

13. REGIONAL WORLD INTEGRATION

Homesteads must function in the same persistent world as villages, roads, travelers, resources, and wildlife.

Required integration

- Real world coordinates
- Valid land ownership and claims
- Persistent buildings
- Functional NPC interactions
- Resource gathering
- Road and trail access
- Regional navigation
- Save/load continuity
- Existing NPC AI
- Existing economy
- Existing mortality and combat
- Appropriate world streaming

When a homestead becomes known to the player, create an appropriate regional map icon.

Do not automatically reveal every remote homestead at world creation.

Do not create duplicate residents when NPCs travel between regions or when world chunks load and unload.

Offscreen simulation

Homesteaders must continue their lives when the player is elsewhere, using the existing offscreen simulation architecture.

Do not require every distant NPC and building to remain fully rendered or physically simulated at all times.

Preserve outcomes and simulation consistency without unnecessary performance costs.

---

14. VALIDATION AND MEASUREMENT

Validate both individual homesteading behavior and long-term population stability.

Functional scenario

Test a complete sequence:

Existing village resident → adulthood or legitimate relocation decision → suitable land selection → physical travel → claim → resource acquisition → shelter construction → independent livelihood → trade with an established settlement.

Verify:

- Persistent identity
- Valid property ownership
- Legitimate construction materials
- Resource and currency conservation
- Functional wilderness interactions
- Save/load continuity
- Correct population accounting
- Normal AI behavior
- Normal danger and mortality

Player scenario

Verify that the player can establish a homestead outside settlement boundaries, gather resources, build, save, reload, and continue living there.

Demographic testing

Run appropriate multigenerational simulation scenarios.

Track:

- Settlement population by age
- Births and deaths
- Young adults reaching household independence
- Migration between settlements
- Homesteading decisions
- Successful and failed homesteads
- Active rural households
- Homesteader mortality
- Abandoned properties
- Settlement population growth or decline
- Rural population as a share of the total

Acceptance criteria

- No systematic depopulation caused by homesteading.
- No artificial population replacement.
- No duplicate NPCs.
- No creation of free goods or money.
- No guaranteed annual migration quota.
- No uncontrolled proliferation of homesteads.
- Homesteaders remain functional NPCs throughout their lives.
- Wilderness buildings and interactions persist.
- Existing demographic, economic, and simulation invariants remain valid.

Use focused validation and reuse passing CI evidence for unaffected systems.

Do not automatically repeat unrelated long-running test campaigns.

16. OWNER AUTHORITY AND FINAL DESIGN RULES

The following decisions are final:

1. Wilderness land outside settlement control may be available for independent homesteading.
2. Both players and existing NPCs may establish homesteads.
3. Homesteading is uncommon and primarily associated with young adults establishing independent households.
4. Established villages must not be progressively emptied.
5. Taxes must not cause mass migration.
6. Homesteaders must physically travel, gather resources, construct homes, and survive through real gameplay.
7. Homesteaders remain fully simulated people with identities, relationships, economic activity, and mortality.
8. Bandits and hostile wildlife present real danger.
9. Families and future generations remain part of the existing demographic system.
10. Homesteads persist in the same living world as all other settlements.

Proceed within these approved requirements without repeatedly requesting authorization.

Ask one focused question only if an essential unresolved design decision prevents safe implementation.

FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

A young villager may grow up, decide that village life is not for them, travel into the countryside, find unclaimed land near useful resources, build a modest home, and begin an independent life.

They may prosper, marry, raise children, trade with nearby settlements, struggle with shortages, encounter bandits, fight off wildlife, or die trying.

Their departure must not undermine the long-term viability of their home village.

The countryside should gradually develop because real people make real decisions—not because the game randomly populates empty land with decorative houses.
