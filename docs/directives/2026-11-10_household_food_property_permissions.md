1. KI-09 — Household Consumption of Business Food

Decision: APPROVED

A food seller's own household may consume food from the seller's business inventory without making a purchase.

A baker should not have a dependent child starving while edible bread sits in the family's own business inventory.

Required behavior

1. Business owners and eligible household members may access edible food from their own business inventory when household food is insufficient.
2. Household consumption is not a commercial sale.
3. No coins change hands.
4. No sales revenue, profit, market activity, or sales tax is generated by the consumption.
5. The exact amount of food consumed or transferred must be deducted from business inventory.
6. Food cannot exist simultaneously in household and business inventory.
7. Consumption must respect existing food availability, ownership, spoilage, and inventory rules.
8. Unrelated households cannot consume another household's business stock without authorization.
9. Preserve the KI-09 self-trade prohibition. Do not simulate a self-purchase to accomplish household consumption.
10. Prevent repeated failed purchase attempts or infinite retry loops.

Investigate the existing household food allocation and business inventory systems before implementing. Use established consumption and transfer mechanisms wherever possible.

Required validation

Test owner consumption, dependent consumption without coins, insufficient inventory, multiple household members competing for limited stock, spoilage restrictions, inventory conservation, save/load persistence, and deterministic replay.

Preserve the six existing self-trade scenarios and their passing results.

The prior validation showed 4 failures before the KI-09 fix and 11 passing checks afterward. The 240-decision no-food scenario must remain free of purchase retry loops.

2. MP-03 — Public Tables and Multiplayer Property Access

Decision: APPROVED WITH THE FOLLOWING RULES

The public-table change on "wip/b25-mp03-public-tables" at "4b83422" is directionally approved, subject to the additional ownership rules below and successful integration testing.

A. Genuinely public tables

Guests may use genuinely public, unowned tables as stores when the existing interaction and reachability rules permit it.

A table must not be considered public merely because it is physically accessible.

Preserve protections for private property, villager inventories, pantries, granaries, unauthorized containers, and carts.

Refused requests must not move goods or coins.

B. Tavern tables

Decision: KEEP PRIVATE.

A tavern is open to customers, but its inventory belongs to the business or its rightful owner.

- Food displayed on tavern tables remains business inventory.
- Normal purchasing and dining interactions remain permitted.
- Unauthorized taking remains theft under the established rules.
- Public access to the tavern does not grant ownership of its goods.

Preserve the existing behavior for all 15 tavern tables in the current village.

C. Player-owned household tables

Decision: ALLOW AUTHORIZED ACCESS.

A multiplayer guest must be able to access tables and containers they own or are otherwise authorized to use, including property in their own home.

- Ownership and permissions determine access.
- Being the multiplayer host does not grant unrestricted ownership.
- Being a guest does not remove legitimate ownership rights.
- Authorized household members should receive access according to existing household permissions.
- Unauthorized players remain blocked.
- The same underlying property rules must apply to host and guest.

Investigate existing ownership, household, and multiplayer permission mechanisms before adding new logic.

D. Tavern tables after the keeper dies

Decision: REMAIN PRIVATE UNTIL OWNERSHIP IS RESOLVED.

The death of a tavern keeper does not automatically make business inventory public.

Determine whether existing inheritance, succession, estate, or property-transfer systems assign a new owner.

If no owner exists, use the established estate or unclaimed-property rules where available.

Do not automatically turn unresolved business property into free public inventory.

If the game lacks a necessary ownership-resolution mechanism, investigate and document the gap before implementing a new system.

3. Multiplayer Security and Authority

All access decisions must be validated by the authoritative host.

A guest must not gain access by modifying a request, impersonating ownership, changing location information, or bypassing interaction rules.

Preserve distance/reachability requirements and the existing security model.

Investigate whether any new owner-specific permission checks introduce a host/guest discrepancy.

Do not weaken private-property protection to enable public-table functionality.
