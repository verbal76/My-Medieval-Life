MY MEDIEVAL LIFE — BUILD 25 ECONOMIC SUSTAINABILITY ANALYSIS

Claude, continue the existing Build 25 year-long validation campaign without interrupting, restarting, or modifying any running tests.

Seed 2 has completed successfully with zero script errors, zero lost coins or items, and population growth from 104 to 279.

The chapel holds 5,147 of 6,566 coins (78.4%), and median household cash is 4 coins. Your comparison against the earlier judgment soak confirms that chapel accumulation and low household liquidity predate the current Expansion 20 integration.

Do not classify these conditions as new regressions without evidence. However, do not dismiss them as economically healthy simply because they are longstanding or the chapel is an approved coin sink.

1. Complete the existing validation

Allow normal-settings Seed 1 to finish, followed by the already scheduled economy-enabled run.

Do not launch duplicate simulations or repeat previously successful tests.

Use the exact source SHA and existing logs for all comparisons.

2. Produce a detailed economic analysis

For each completed 365-day simulation, extract and graph the available checkpoints for:

- Total population, births, deaths, immigration, emigration, and household formation.
- Working-age population, employment, unemployment, occupations, and available jobs.
- Total coins, household cash, institutional holdings, business cash, and merchant cash.
- Chapel treasury accumulation, incoming donations, outgoing payments, relief spending, and net cash flow.
- Median household wealth, distribution of cash, and households with insufficient purchasing power.
- Production, consumption, inventories, shortages, prices, and completed trades.
- Business formation, business failures, wages, profitability, and worker hiring.
- Bandit activity, stolen goods, losses, and economic consequences.
- Judgment and reputation effects on trading, employment, cooperation, and migration.

If a metric is not recorded, explicitly identify it as unavailable. Do not invent values or imply that a metric was measured when it was not.

3. Separate three different forms of economic health

A. Conservation and technical integrity

Verify that coins and items are conserved, transactions are accounted for, and there are no script errors, invalid inventories, duplication, or unexplained losses.

B. Money circulation

Determine whether money is moving among households, businesses, merchants, the chapel, and civic institutions.

Specifically investigate whether chapel accumulation is removing too much currency from ordinary economic circulation.

Identify the actual mechanisms through which chapel funds return to the village, including alms, relief, purchases, employment, and other approved expenditures.

C. Long-term sustainability

Determine whether employment opportunities, wages, production, trade, and purchasing power keep pace with population growth.

Identify whether the village can sustain itself economically or is developing structural unemployment, persistent poverty, resource shortages, or institutional wealth concentration.

Do not treat population growth alone as proof of economic sustainability.

4. Compare historical and current results

Use the earlier judgment soak and any other compatible completed year-long simulations as baselines.

Distinguish:

- Longstanding design behavior.
- Changes introduced by Expansion 20.
- Differences caused by simulation settings.
- Differences attributable to seeds.
- Potential defects.
- Potential balancing concerns.

Do not claim a causal relationship without supporting evidence.

5. Compare economy-disabled and economy-enabled behavior

When the economy-enabled run completes, compare it against the normal-settings runs.

Determine whether the Living Economy improves or worsens:

- Employment and economic participation.
- Household purchasing power.
- Market activity and goods circulation.
- Institutional wealth concentration.
- Supply and demand responses.
- Settlement sustainability.
- NPC autonomy and economic decision-making.

Clearly identify which Living Economy features were actually enabled in each simulation.

6. Deliverables

Produce a consolidated report containing:

1. Executive summary.
2. Per-seed results and checkpoint graphs.
3. Historical baseline comparisons.
4. Coin conservation and circulation analysis.
5. Employment and population analysis.
6. Production, trade, and household sustainability analysis.
7. Bandit and judgment findings.
8. Economy-disabled versus economy-enabled comparison.
9. Identified defects, design limitations, and balancing concerns.
10. Recommended corrective actions, ranked by severity and supported by evidence.

Include actual measurements, source logs, commit SHAs, and reproducible analysis scripts.

Distinguish technical validation passed from economic sustainability demonstrated.

7. Development restrictions

This is an analysis and reporting assignment only.

Do not alter chapel behavior, employment, population growth, monetary distribution, pricing, or other gameplay systems without my approval.

Do not change the validating Build 25 source.

Do not activate the Living Economy in normal gameplay.

Do not launch additional long-running campaigns without identifying a specific evidence gap and obtaining authorization.

You may continue preparing the analysis while existing simulations run.

8. Reporting

Keep the existing simulations running uninterrupted.

When the remaining runs finish, deliver the consolidated comparative report. If any run fails, investigate and report the cause before proposing a rerun.

Our objective is to determine whether My Medieval Life has a functioning, sustainable living economy—not merely whether its simulation can run for 365 days without crashing.
