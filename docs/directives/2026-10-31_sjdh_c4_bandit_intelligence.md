EXPANSION 20 — SJD-H / C4: BANDIT SURVIVAL, RETALIATION, AND PURSUIT INTELLIGENCE

Do not automatically reduce bandit mortality or weaken villagers' willingness to defend themselves and their families.

The owner's design intention is that villagers may become angry, organize, pursue, and kill bandits responsible for robbery, assault, or murder. This is legitimate emergent behavior arising from the Social Judgment and Decision system.

However, bandits must possess comparable situational awareness, threat assessment, and self-preservation capabilities.

Required behavior:

1. Bandits must recognize when they are being pursued, threatened, surrounded, or significantly outnumbered. Recognition must depend on information reasonably available to them, not omniscient knowledge.

2. Bandits must anticipate possible retaliation based on their actions, previous experiences, witnessed events, and information communicated by other bandits.

3. Being hunted should increase tactical aggression where appropriate. A cornered bandit may become more willing to fight, attack an escape-blocking opponent, or use violence to create an opportunity to retreat.

4. Tactical aggression must never be implemented through arbitrary increases to health, damage, speed, or combat statistics.

5. Bandits must evaluate whether to fight, flee, hide, regroup, defend companions, or attempt an ambush. Decisions should incorporate numerical advantage, equipment, injuries, stamina, personality, relationships, and escape opportunities.

6. Bandits should cooperate when circumstances justify it. They may defend fellow bandits, regroup against pursuers, or cover another bandit's retreat. Cooperation should depend on relationships, awareness, and individual motivations rather than automatic universal loyalty.

7. Bandits should not repeatedly engage in hopeless combat simply because their existing AI cannot transition into a retreat or survival response.

8. Villagers should remain capable of organizing successful retaliation. Do not weaken family-defense behavior simply to produce a lower bandit death count.

9. The same underlying judgment principles must apply to both populations. Bandits are individuals making decisions based on circumstances, not disposable enemies programmed to die.

10. Bandits should learn from encounters through the existing memory, judgment, and experience systems where supported. Avoid introducing an isolated AI subsystem that bypasses established mechanics.

Important distinction:

Increased aggression means greater willingness to use violence when threatened. It does not mean every hunted bandit blindly charges at pursuers. An intelligent retreat, regrouping maneuver, or defensive ambush may be a more appropriate aggressive survival response.

Validation requirements:

Before changing combat balance, investigate the existing 365-day simulation results showing 143–234 bandit deaths with judgment enabled.

Determine:

- How many deaths occur during active robberies or assaults.
- How many occur during organized retaliation or pursuit.
- How many occur after bandits have disengaged.
- Whether bandits recognize numerical disadvantages.
- Whether bandits attempt to retreat.
- Whether retreat attempts succeed or fail.
- Whether bandits cooperate defensively.
- Whether repetitive robbery behavior artificially increases combat encounters.
- Whether bandit respawning artificially inflates annual mortality.

After implementing the required behavioral improvements, use controlled encounters and appropriate simulations to measure survival, successful retreats, defensive cooperation, pursuit outcomes, and behavioral variety.

Do not impose an arbitrary target bandit death rate.

The objective is believable, intelligent behavior on both sides of a conflict—not artificial numerical balance.

Keep the work consistent with existing Expansion 20 integration and regression requirements. Do not alter the frozen Build 24 release.

Report findings, behavioral changes, and validation evidence before declaring C4 resolved.
