MY MEDIEVAL LIFE — CHAPEL ECONOMIC PURPOSE CORRECTION

OWNER DIRECTIVE — BUILD 25

Claude, I want to clarify the fundamental purpose of the chapel in My Medieval Life.

The chapel is a charitable institution serving the village. It is not intended to accumulate money and food indefinitely while villagers struggle or starve.

Think of it as a community savings and emergency assistance institution.

Its purpose is to collect voluntary donations and produce food, maintain reasonable operating reserves, and redistribute resources where they are genuinely needed.

1. FOOD DISTRIBUTION IS A PRIMARY PURPOSE

The chapel's donated food and agricultural production exist primarily to feed people experiencing hardship.

Preserve only food legitimately needed for:

- Immediate clergy and staff consumption.
- Livestock under the chapel's care, if any.
- Necessary seed for future agricultural production.
- A modest, justified emergency reserve.

Everything beyond those needs should be available for charitable distribution.

The chapel must not stockpile excessive food while villagers are starving.

Preserve the existing 14-day household hardship assessment for ordinary charitable eligibility.

Distinguish that assessment from the existing emergency food trigger. Households facing imminent hunger must receive higher priority and faster assistance.

Implement proactive identification of households needing food. Do not rely exclusively on a starving villager successfully walking to the chapel.

Use legitimate delivery, collection, and transfer mechanisms. No teleporting food or inventing supplies.

Prioritize the most urgent need, especially households with children, elders, or people already missing meals.

Ensure available supplies are distributed fairly rather than exhausting everything on the first qualifying household.

Food must remain subject to the normal spoilage system.

Distribute older usable food before it spoils.

2. CHAPEL FINANCES

The chapel should retain enough money to sustain legitimate operations, including:

- Clergy wages and existing wage obligations.
- Essential supplies and agricultural operations.
- Necessary repairs and maintenance.
- Modest operating reserves.
- Approved future improvements when an actual project and cost exist.

Do not accumulate excessive speculative reserves for upgrades that have not been designed or authorized.

Use the existing approved operating-reserve framework as the starting point. Audit whether its calculations retain more money than reasonably necessary.

Money beyond legitimate operating needs should support the community.

Preserve voluntary donations. Do not impoverish households through excessive collections.

3. CHARITABLE ASSISTANCE

The chapel should provide assistance through two complementary programs.

Food relief: Families who cannot obtain adequate food receive appropriate food assistance.

Financial assistance: Households experiencing genuine hardship can receive money to cover essential needs, according to the established hardship and alms rules.

The chapel must not reserve thousands of coins while qualifying impoverished households lack the means to survive.

Emergency relief takes precedence over discretionary projects.

Assistance must remain finite, accountable, and based on available resources.

4. COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT AND LENDING

I am also approving the chapel's broader purpose as a potential source of community financial support.

Eventually, it should be able to support:

- Families recovering from serious hardship.
- Viable businesses experiencing temporary financial difficulties.
- Legitimate village improvement projects.
- Necessary community infrastructure.
- Emergency assistance during economic downturns.

This may include charitable grants and repayable loans.

The concept of chapel-supported community lending is approved.

However, inspect existing loan, credit, property, and repayment systems before implementing it.

Do not invent interest rates, collateral seizure, repayment schedules, or debt enforcement without an approved design.

Prepare a separate implementation proposal for lending using existing economic systems wherever possible.

The immediate food and financial relief corrections must not be delayed by this future feature.

5. INVESTIGATE STARVATION DEATHS

We have repeatedly investigated starvation and food insecurity in our simulations.

I want a specific investigation of whether anyone dies from hunger while the chapel holds usable food or sufficient money to purchase available food.

For each such death, identify:

- The household's actual food and financial position.
- Available chapel food and funds.
- Whether the household qualified for assistance.
- Whether assistance was offered or requested.
- Whether delivery was attempted.
- Whether timing, prioritization, pathfinding, eligibility, or another defect prevented assistance.

Correct verified implementation defects.

Do not guarantee survival when supplies genuinely do not exist.

However, preventable starvation caused by charitable food sitting unused in storage is unacceptable.

6. MEASURE THE RESULTS

Instrument the chapel's entire economic cycle:

- Voluntary food and coin donations.
- Food harvested.
- Food consumed by legitimate operations.
- Food distributed to households.
- Food spoiled.
- Financial alms.
- Operating expenses.
- Fund and food reserves.
- Households requesting aid.
- Households receiving aid.
- Qualified households not helped, with reasons.
- Starvation deaths involving households eligible for chapel assistance.

Use focused tests and representative simulations to compare conditions before and after the corrections.

Preserve currency conservation, inventory conservation, deterministic simulation, and save compatibility.

Do not automatically launch multiple expensive year-long simulations.

7. DEVELOPMENT INSTRUCTIONS

Record this as an owner-approved clarification and expansion of the chapel's purpose.

Preserve existing legitimate chapel, agricultural, religious, and charitable systems.

Integrate the worker's verified spoilage and surplus-food corrections.

Keep the 14-day hardship assessment consistent across food and financial aid, while preserving faster emergency intervention for imminent starvation.

Continue other approved Build 25 work while these corrections are developed and tested.

Document any genuine lending-policy questions separately rather than stopping unrelated development.

The standard for success is simple:

The chapel should accumulate resources when the village is prosperous and use those resources when villagers need help.

It should maintain itself, not enrich itself.

When people are hungry, charitable food should feed them.

When families face hardship, available charitable funds should help them.

And when the village recovers, the chapel should rebuild its reserves for the next difficult period.

Proceed with the immediate corrections and report the results.
