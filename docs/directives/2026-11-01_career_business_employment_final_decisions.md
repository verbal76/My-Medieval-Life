1. RECORD THE FOUR APPROVED OWNER DECISIONS

Decision 1 — Career advancement

APPROVED:

Experience, ability, ambition, and available opportunities determine career advancement. There are no automatic promotions.

NPCs should improve their professional capabilities through actual work and training.

As they develop, they should recognize opportunities to advance their careers.

Career advancement must consider:

- Experience gained through relevant work.
- Actual professional ability.
- Individual ambitions and aspirations.
- Available employment opportunities.
- Employer needs and hiring decisions.
- Economic circumstances.
- Resources and tools.
- Training and apprenticeship opportunities.
- Individual willingness to pursue advancement.

An NPC must not automatically become a master simply because a fixed experience or skill threshold has been reached.

Existing owner decision X18-D3, which rejected automatic skill-threshold promotions, remains in force.

Skill and experience may establish competence or eligibility, but advancement must involve appropriate opportunities and NPC decisions.

Professional titles must correspond to meaningful differences in responsibilities, capabilities, work opportunities, or economic roles where the approved profession system calls for them.

Do not introduce arbitrary universal promotion schedules.

Decision 2 — Independent businesses

APPROVED:

Qualified villagers may establish independent businesses when they have the resources, motivation, and economic opportunity to do so.

NPCs must be capable of pursuing independent economic activity.

Business formation should consider:

- Relevant occupational ability and experience.
- Personal aspirations.
- Available resources and capital.
- Necessary tools and materials.
- Suitable premises or production facilities.
- Demand for products or services.
- Existing market competition.
- Household financial circumstances.
- Ability to sustain the business.
- Applicable existing ownership, building, and economic rules.

Business ownership must not be artificially restricted to the starting set of NPCs.

A qualified, motivated villager should be able to pursue a business opportunity through the game's actual systems.

However, opening a business is not guaranteed to succeed.

Businesses may struggle, expand, change ownership, or close through normal economic processes.

Preserve existing owner-approved rules for construction, property, resources, production, and trade.

Decision 3 — Skilled employment growth

APPROVED:

Businesses hire and train workers according to production needs and affordability, without fixed headcount caps.

Remove the artificial restriction that prevents skilled occupations from growing beyond their initial staffing levels.

This includes the previously identified starting populations of smiths, carpenters, tanners, cooks, and merchants.

Do not replace the old fixed limits with another arbitrary cap.

Actual employment should be constrained by legitimate conditions, including:

- Available work.
- Product demand.
- Employer resources.
- Wage affordability.
- Workplace capacity.
- Available tools and materials.
- Worker qualifications.
- Training capacity.
- Market conditions.
- Business viability.

An expanding business should be capable of employing additional workers.

A struggling business may reduce employment or close.

New businesses should create additional employment opportunities when economically justified.

Employment growth should emerge naturally from the settlement's development.

Do not manufacture jobs merely to reduce unemployment statistics.

Decision 4 — Activate career ambition

APPROVED:

Integrate and activate the existing skill-aware NPC career ambition system, correcting any defects.

This resolves the relevant activation decision under X19-SWITCHES-D1 for the career ambition system.

Do not interpret this as blanket authorization to activate unrelated experimental systems or feature switches.

Inspect the existing ambition implementation before changing it.

Preserve and reuse approved functionality wherever possible.

Correct known skill mappings, including the reported carpenter, tanner, farmer, and stonecutter issues, after verifying the correct relationships against the actual profession definitions.

Connect NPC aspirations to real career decisions.

NPCs should be able to recognize that their growing experience and abilities make them candidates for better opportunities.

They should then decide whether to pursue those opportunities according to their individual circumstances.

Not every NPC must be ambitious.

Not every ambitious NPC must succeed.

Do not replace individual decision-making with mandatory promotions.

Connect the relevant existing systems for:

- Professional experience.
- Business aspirations.
- Resource ownership.
- Tools and equipment.
- Building or workplace availability.
- Production.
- Market demand.
- Trading and procurement.
- Business finances.
- Hiring and employment.

A successful independent business may expand its workforce.

A master craftsman may train apprentices or employ qualified workers.

Independent producers should participate in the normal economy rather than receiving artificial resources or guaranteed customers.

Preserve currency and item conservation.

Business creation must not manufacture money, materials, or inventory.

6. REMOVE ARTIFICIAL SKILLED-JOB STAGNATION

The career audit found that skilled-trade headcounts remain fixed at their initial values while the adult population expands.

Correct this structural limitation.

Skilled jobs must be capable of appearing through legitimate economic activity.

The open-job system must recognize suitable opportunities created by existing and new businesses.

Hiring must consider the actual skills and suitability of candidates.

Employment must not be limited to replacing deceased, retired, or departed original workers.

Avoid guaranteed employment, fixed population-based job quotas, or artificial unemployment corrections.

The economy should be capable of growth, stagnation, and contraction depending on actual circumstances.
