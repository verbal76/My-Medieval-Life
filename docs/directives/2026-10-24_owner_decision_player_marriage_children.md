MY MEDIEVAL LIFE — OWNER DECISION: CHILDREN IN PLAYER-TO-PLAYER MARRIAGES

The outstanding question concerning children in marriages between two player characters is now resolved.

This is a final owner design decision. Record it and proceed accordingly.

1. Biological children

When two player characters are married:

- Male + Female: They may have biological children organically through the game's existing reproduction and family mechanics.
- Male + Male: Biological children are not available to the couple. They may adopt.
- Female + Female: Biological children are not available to the couple. They may adopt.

Do not introduce artificial reproduction methods or alternative biological parentage mechanics.

2. Adoption

Same-sex married couples must have access to adoption.

Adopted children must be recognized as legitimate members of the family, with both spouses recorded as parents.

Apply the existing family relationship, household, inheritance, and social systems consistently to adopted children.

Do not invent additional restrictions or penalties for adoption.

3. Shared parenthood

For both biological and adopted children:

- Both player spouses must be recognized as parents.
- Children must recognize both parents and their family relationships.
- Existing household, relationship, inheritance, and NPC social systems must recognize the family.
- Children remain persistent members of the shared world when either or both players are offline.
- Parenthood and family relationships must survive saving, loading, hosting, and reconnecting.

4. Implementation boundaries

Reuse the existing NPC marriage, reproduction, adoption, and family systems wherever possible.

Do not build a separate reproduction or family simulation exclusively for multiplayer.

Do not introduce new pregnancy timing, fertility rates, adoption costs, age restrictions, or other mechanics without checking the existing design first.

Preserve deterministic simulation behavior and multiplayer host authority.

5. Project tracking

Record this decision in the appropriate design documentation and recovery handoff.

Close the outstanding player-to-player children design question as OWNER DECISION RESOLVED.

Distinguish between:

- Design decision resolved.
- Implementation completed.
- Implementation tested.

Do not mark implementation complete unless the relevant functionality actually exists and has been verified.

If implementation is already within the current approved multiplayer scope, proceed using these rules. Otherwise, place the implementation in the appropriate backlog without expanding the current work campaign.

Do not reopen this decision unless a genuine technical conflict requires owner input.

6. Report back

Briefly confirm:

1. Where the decision was recorded.
2. Whether existing systems already support the required behavior.
3. What implementation work, if any, remains.
4. Whether the blocker is now cleared.

Keep the report concise. Do not launch a new testing campaign or unrelated development work.

Owner decision is final.
