1. OWNER DECISION — B25-BIZ-TENANT-Q

OPTION B IS APPROVED.

A villager who rents their residence may establish a standalone workshop on legally purchased land.

This is consistent with my existing November 1 Decision 5.

A person does not need to own their residence to own a business.

The distinctions are:

A. HOME-ATTACHED WORKSHOPS

A household that owns its home may expand that property by building suitable attached commercial additions.

These include the existing workshop, storeroom, shop, storefront, and other supported building arrangements.

These additions must continue using the existing modular construction, property, access, and economic systems.

This functionality is explicitly preserved. Do not remove, replace, or restrict it.

B. STANDALONE BUSINESSES

A household may establish a standalone workshop on separately acquired land even if its residence is rented.

Requirements:

- Suitable existing housing.
- Legally acquired business land.
- Sufficient genuine financial resources.
- Appropriate skill and experience.
- Actual construction materials.
- Valid construction and placement.
- Working capital.
- Legitimate economic opportunity.
- The household's voluntary business-founding decision.

The household owns its business property but does not acquire ownership of its rented residence.

C. PURCHASING EXISTING BUSINESSES

Eligible villagers may purchase workshops offered for sale by willing owners.

Purchasing an existing workshop does not require ownership of the purchaser's residence.

Honor the existing property-transfer, business-finance, consent, and inventory rules.

D. HOME STOREFRONTS AND SMALL SHOPS

Preserve my previously approved system allowing a homeowner to develop their residence into a small commercial operation.

That includes the existing home storefront against the house, commercial additions, shops established in suitable rooms, and workshops attached to owned residential property.

Do not confuse these with standalone commercial construction.

The ability to build a shop or workshop at home must remain available.

E. HOUSING FIRST

My original "a house first, then workshops" rule means a person must have legitimate, suitable housing before pursuing nonessential commercial construction.

An owned home satisfies the requirement.

A legitimately rented home also satisfies the housing requirement for separately owned standalone commercial property.

A tenant does not thereby gain permission to construct additions to a landlord's house.

A villager without suitable housing must address that need before building a business.

5. Available housing.
6. Residential ownership versus tenancy.
7. Available commercial land.
8. Workshop construction feasibility.
9. Household capital.
10. Business startup costs.
11. Required working capital.
12. Available construction materials.
13. Demand for each profession.
14. Existing production capacity.
15. Available workshops for purchase.
16. Actual selling opportunities.
17. Judgment and ambition decisions.
