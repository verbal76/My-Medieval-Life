Status: owner-directed concept approved in principle • Implementation build: to be scheduled after
current stabilization • Companion: Mining & Industrial Development Expansion


1. Executive design decision
Separate the progression of villages into towns and major settlements, and the regional trade system
that results, from the dedicated Mining & Industrial Development Expansion. Mining develops what
an industry can do; this expansion develops what a settlement collectively knows, produces, needs,
trades, and becomes.

Permanent design rule: a caravan is not an automatic daily income source. The quantity of money
and goods arriving depends on whether another settlement needs what is offered, can afford it, and
finds the trade worthwhile. A settlement with no competitive or useful export may receive little or
no outside money.

For Builds 15–16, the current 30-coin daily caravan allowance may remain an explicitly labeled,
adjustable temporary simulation stand-in. It is not an approved permanent economic rule. Do not
disrupt ongoing equilibrium validation to implement this expansion.

3. Settlement growth is emergent, not an upgrade button
Settlements may grow from hamlets/villages to towns and major settlements through sustained
population, homes, civic capacity, economic output, services and trade connections. These labels
describe development, not universal tech tiers. Growth is not guaranteed or permanent: shortages,
migration, disasters, poor governance and failed industries can cause stagnation or decline.

     No automatic technology unlock merely because a population threshold is crossed.
     No universal ladder where every town has the same industries. A small mining village can
      outclass a larger farming town in mining.

4. Industry capability levels belong to settlements
Each settlement maintains an industry capability profile, derived from the actual capabilities of its
residents and infrastructure. Individual NPC skills remain distinct; settlement proficiency does not
magically grant expertise to every villager.

These are approved mining progression concepts; specific thresholds and cross-industry
prerequisites are to be designed and tested, not assumed fixed. An advanced mining operation may
depend on capable smiths, carpenters, haulers and builders.

Capability progression mechanics
     Experience: successful work, practice, apprenticeships and problem-solving produce knowledge
      and competence.
     Specialists: qualified NPCs enable advanced activities; recruiting or training them takes time
      and resources.
     Infrastructure: appropriate workshops, storage, transport, tools and construction must exist and
      remain functional.
     Knowledge continuity: instruction, apprenticeship and records preserve skills across
      generations; population loss can erode capacity.
     Industry interdependence: advanced operations require actual outputs of supporting
      professions, not abstract research points.
     Settlement specialization: local geology, land, labor, demand and investment encourage
      distinctive economies.


5. Regional economy replaces the caravan quota
A caravan represents an originating settlement or trade network. Ultimately it carries actual goods
and finite purchasing power based on external inventories, needs and means. It must not buy every
available product by default.

A viable trade transaction requires
     Demand: a destination settlement has a meaningful unmet need or profitable resale
      opportunity.
     Supply: the source settlement has real goods available after its own competing uses and
      commitments.
     Purchasing power: the buyer has money or acceptable barter/credit terms; need alone does not
      create wealth.
     Price and competition: total cost, quality, distance, risks and alternative suppliers make the
      trade worthwhile.
     Logistics: cart capacity, roads, travel time, losses, security and storage constrain quantity.
     Accounting: physical inventory and currency move between actual owners. No hidden
      duplication or infinite cash.

7. Caravans: transition from placeholder to simulated merchant

Current phase: Builds 15–16
     Retain the provisional 30-coin/day mechanism only as needed for baseline stability; label it
      clearly as an abstract external-market budget.
     Measure net coin inflow/outflow, import/export quantities, inventory conservation and
      dependence on imported ore/coal.
     Keep the budget configurable and expose its effects in simulation reports; do not silently treat it
      as real demand.

Intermediate phase: regional-market abstraction
     Create abstract off-map settlements with distinct finite stocks, needs, productive strengths and
      budgets.
     Generate caravan orders from modeled demand and purchasing power rather than a fixed daily
      purchasing obligation.

     Use explicit restocking and settlement-accounting rules; avoid unexplained daily currency
      creation.
     Support variable arrivals and empty trips when no worthwhile trade exists.

Mature phase: fully simulated regional settlements
     Trade routes link actual settlements with persistent populations, production, consumption,
      inventories and treasuries.
     Merchants select profitable or necessary routes subject to competition and transport capacity.
     Regional supply disruptions and new industrial skills change prices, routes and settlement
      fortunes.
     The player can participate in or disrupt these relationships without becoming the central
      economic engine.


8. Settlement growth systems and their interlocks
     Population and housing: births, partnerships, households, migration, jobs, construction
      materials and capacity must agree.
     Industry: training and specialist availability determine what can be made and exported.
     Infrastructure: roads, bridges, warehouses, marketplaces, workshops and carts affect costs and
      throughput.
     Civic systems: leadership, budgets, public works and security may enable or hinder growth,
      using finite funds.
     Ecology and land: farming, forests, mines, quarries and wildlife remain finite and affected by
      local activity.
     Knowledge and generations: experience can be taught, recorded or lost; no omniscient
      settlement skill bonus.
     Reputation and diplomacy: reliability, conflict and trade history may affect partners, subject to
      knowledge provenance.


9. Failure modes and economic safeguards
     Do not equate settlement tier with every industry level; avoid uniform cookie-cutter cities.
     Do not award skills solely by time elapsed or abstract points disconnected from work.
     Do not grant free imports, money, ore, food, tools or building materials to keep an economy
      looking healthy.
     Avoid permanent guaranteed export demand; settlements can lose customers and decline.
     Prevent circular money printing via merchant budgets, resale, taxes or trade accounting.
     Allow local setbacks without declaring the entire world failed; test adaptation and long-term
      survival.
     Keep mining deposits finite. Continued mining employment depends on discovery, new
      workings, trade and real labor.

11. Acceptance criteria and simulation gates
     A village can prosper or decline without direct player intervention, with causes traceable to real
      inputs.
     At least two settlements can develop different industry profiles despite different population
      sizes.
     A settlement cannot perform advanced work without appropriate people, knowledge, tools and
      infrastructure.
     A caravan may arrive with substantial buying power when real demand and funds exist, or buy
      nothing when demand is absent.
     Imported and exported goods and money reconcile exactly across merchant and settlement
      inventories.
     A starving but cash-poor town does not conjure purchasing power; trade failure is observable.
     No fixed 30-coin quota remains in the final demand-driven implementation.
     Multi-seed 100-day, 365-day and multi-generation tests examine production, food, housing,
      population, industry employment, prices, trade and resilience.
     Existing save games and established gameplay systems continue to work; migration is
      documented.


12. Decisions settled and questions deferred

Approved direction
     Mining/industrial extraction is its own expansion, not an emergency patch to Builds 15–16.
     Settlement advancement and regional economy is a separate, connected expansion.
     Settlement capabilities are industry-specific and earned through people, skills and
      infrastructure.
     Caravan buying power ultimately comes from other settlements’ actual needs and means, not a
      permanent allowance.
     Current 30-coin daily mechanism is a temporary placeholder only.

13. Claude handoff and change-control directive
Register this expansion as a distinct major initiative in the permanent requirements ledger and
Interlocking Systems Manifest. Cross-reference, but do not merge it into, the Mining & Industrial
Development Expansion. Preserve the owner’s decisions verbatim in decision records. First audit
existing economy, professions, caravan code, settlement representation, persistence and simulation
harness. Then propose phased implementation with testable requirements, dependencies and
migration plans. Continue currently approved Build 15–16 stabilization and simulations without
introducing this large feature prematurely.

Design principle: a settlement becomes economically important because of what its people can
sustainably produce and what other settlements genuinely need—not because a game system grants
it a daily customer.
