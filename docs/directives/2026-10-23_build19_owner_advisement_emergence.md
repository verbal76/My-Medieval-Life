MY MEDIEVAL LIFE — OWNER ADVISEMENT FOR BUILD 19

This is a design and execution clarification, not a new development assignment.

Do not interrupt the active worker, launch new agents, start additional tests, or change the approved Build 19 schedule. Continue the authorized work.

1. Integration is a task, not inherently a risk

Every expansion introduces overlapping systems. We expect integration conflicts, behavioral changes, and occasional defects.

These are normal development tasks.

Reserve the term risk for credible threats to project integrity, recoverability, or delivery that cannot be adequately controlled through our existing development practices.

Do not escalate routine integration work simply because two systems interact or require corrections.

2. The simulation is supposed to change

Build 19 introduces personality, judgment, relationships, reputation, and individual decision-making.

These systems are supposed to influence the economy, survival, employment, families, crime, and community behavior.

Do not attempt to preserve Build 18 outcomes merely because they were previously observed.

The old baseline remains historical evidence, not a target for reproducing identical village behavior.

We expect different outcomes when we change the rules.

3. Unexpected outcomes are not automatically defects

My Medieval Life is a living world, not a scripted experience.

A village may prosper, collapse, recover, become divided, or experience extraordinary cooperation.

A family may become wealthy or lose everything.

A villager may make a terrible decision because of pride, anger, loyalty, desperation, or misinformation.

The player may intervene, ignore the situation, make it worse, or simply watch events unfold.

All of these are potentially legitimate outcomes.

Before correcting unexpected behavior, distinguish:

- Emergent behavior: Unexpected but explainable consequences of functioning systems. Preserve it.
- Actual defect: Broken rules, invalid decisions, corrupted state, impossible transactions, lost identity, conservation violations, or other demonstrable implementation failures. Correct it.
- Design question: A technically valid outcome that raises a genuine question about the intended rules. Present the evidence for owner review rather than silently rebalancing it.

Do not introduce artificial rescues, hidden resources, forced prosperity, guaranteed survival, or scripted recovery to make the simulation look healthier.

4. Player agency includes inaction

The world must continue independently of the player.

The player is not obligated to save a village, rescue a family, prevent a crime, or solve an economic crisis.

NPCs must continue making their own decisions and responding to circumstances.

The player can be a hero, a villain, an ordinary resident, a passive observer, or someone simply struggling to survive.

Inaction may have consequences when other characters can reasonably observe and interpret it, but it must not trigger artificial punishment merely because the player declined to intervene.

5. Verification philosophy

The objective of testing is to establish that the simulation follows its rules, not that every outcome is pleasant or familiar.

Verify determinism, resource conservation, permanent identity, save integrity, decision validity, and the approved feature behavior.

Use the flight recorder to explain significant changes.

Do not turn every unusual death, bankruptcy, conflict, or economic shift into a defect ticket without evidence of a broken rule.

Preserve the Build 18 baseline. Propose the new Build 19 baseline with documented, explainable differences for owner approval.

Reuse existing passing evidence where applicable. Do not initiate redundant test campaigns.

6. Release execution

Complete all approved Build 19 functionality, including active personality behavior, family reputation, inherited stories, deliberate lies, family sacrifice, and the supporting systems.

No partial Build 19 release that defers approved functionality to 19.x.

Keep the current resource allocation. The existing completion worker remains on the critical path.

Proceed with the already-authorized integration and verification sequence when the worker finishes. Do not publish or initiate an owner-gated release without authorization.

No response or additional status report is necessary unless this advisement conflicts with an existing approved requirement or reveals a genuine blocker.

Continue development.

Foundational principle

My Medieval Life does not promise the player a good life. It provides a world governed by consistent rules, where every character has agency, circumstances have consequences, and meaningful stories emerge without being scripted.

Sometimes the world is wonderful. Sometimes it is cruel. Sometimes the player changes everything. Sometimes the player does nothing.

And sometimes Nero just fiddles while Rome burns.

That is intentional game design, not a defect.
