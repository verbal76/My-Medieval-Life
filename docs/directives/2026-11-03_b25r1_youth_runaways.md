B25R1 — OWNER DIRECTIVE: YOUTH RUNAWAYS AND BANDIT RECRUITMENT

Extend the event-driven NPC life-decision framework to recognize young people who leave their households because of persistent hardship.

1. Causes and motivation

A child or adolescent may consider running away when their household experiences sustained problems such as:

- Chronic food shortages and hunger
- Persistent poverty or inability to meet basic needs
- Repeated household instability
- Neglect or serious family conflict, where supported by existing simulation data
- A lack of realistic improvement in their circumstances

One bad day or one missed meal must not automatically trigger flight.

Use accumulated conditions and the existing NPC judgment, relationship, needs, and household systems rather than a simple random roll.

2. Event-driven decisions

Evaluate runaway decisions when meaningful hardship thresholds or life events occur, not every simulation tick.

Distinguish among:

- Remaining home and enduring hardship
- Seeking assistance from relatives or other residents
- Seeking work or shelter elsewhere
- Running away to another settlement
- Attempting independent survival
- Becoming involved with an existing bandit group

Age, maturity, available opportunities, personal circumstances, and existing judgment must influence the outcome.

3. Joining bandits

Running away must not automatically turn someone into a bandit.

A runaway may encounter an existing bandit group and seek to join it, particularly when other survival opportunities are unavailable.

Bandit recruitment must use actual NPC identities and existing faction, combat, reputation, and mortality systems.

- Do not spawn a replacement bandit.
- Preserve the runaway's identity, age, skills, relationships, and history.
- Joining a bandit group must be a distinct decision or event.
- Bandits may reject, exploit, or accept a potential recruit according to their existing behavior.
- Children must not automatically become capable adult fighters.
- Recruitment must not grant free equipment, combat skills, or money.
- A runaway may later leave the bandit group or return to ordinary society if existing systems permit it.

4. Family and settlement consequences

The original household must retain the runaway's identity and relationship records.

Where supported, family members may notice the absence, search for the runaway, or react emotionally.

The runaway must remain accounted for in the world's population. Do not duplicate or erase them when they leave home or change affiliations.

5. Population safeguards

Do not turn household poverty into an automatic mechanism that empties villages.

Runaways should be uncommon, consequential events arising from sustained hardship.

Track the frequency of runaways, their destinations, survival, bandit recruitment, and possible return.

6. Integration with homesteading

Keep these decisions distinct:

- Homesteading: A person voluntarily establishes an independent residence, usually during adulthood or household formation.
- Running away: A young person leaves an existing household because of sustained hardship.
- Bandit recruitment: A separate potential outcome following contact with a bandit group.

All three should share the existing identity, travel, household, judgment, and population systems without becoming interchangeable behaviors.

Audit the existing childhood, household hardship, bandit membership, and NPC decision systems before implementation. Reuse them wherever possible.

Document the decision process and validate it through focused scenarios, including a hardship case that does not produce a runaway and one that legitimately does.
