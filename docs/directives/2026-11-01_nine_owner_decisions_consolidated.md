PART 1 — NINE APPROVED OWNER DECISIONS

DECISION 1 — ORGANIC SKILLS, APPRENTICESHIP, AND CAREER DEVELOPMENT

APPROVED: Learning through actual experience, without mandatory professional credentials.

Characters develop abilities through relevant activities regardless of their current professions.

A villager who mines stone to build his own house gains Mining experience even if he has never worked professionally as a miner.

That experience may eventually qualify him for mining employment or independent production.

Requirements:

1. Skills advance through actual relevant activity, training, experimentation, and applicable learning opportunities.
2. Personal projects contribute to skills just as paid work does.
3. Professional titles must not artificially restrict demonstrated abilities.
4. Apprentice, journeyman, and master may describe experience or occupational standing, but they are not universal mandatory licenses.
5. Apprenticeship is an optional training pathway, not the exclusive route to competence.
6. Skilled characters may pursue other professions when opportunities arise.
7. Career advancement considers ability, ambition, circumstances, opportunity, and willingness.
8. No automatic professional promotion based solely on reaching a skill threshold.
9. Preserve individual NPC judgment and legitimate employer decisions.

The individual learns through living, not merely through following a predetermined career.

Implement this using the existing career, experience, aspiration, hiring, and employment systems.

Do not introduce a competing progression framework.

DECISION 2 — INFORMAL SERVICES AND EXPANDED SERVICE CONTRACTS

APPROVED: Both informal skilled services and the previously authorized broader service-contract categories.

A character does not need to own a business or hold a professional title to offer a service they can competently perform.

For example, someone capable of repairing a piece of equipment may offer that service to another person, whether for payment or voluntarily.

Requirements:

1. Any capable and willing character may offer an applicable service.
2. Skill, equipment, available resources, time, and legitimate access determine feasibility.
3. Services may be compensated through negotiated payment or provided without payment.
4. Informal work must not automatically require an official business or formal contract.
5. Existing formal service contracts remain available where appropriate.
6. Incorporate the previously approved CON-03 through CON-08 service categories and their relevant dependencies into the Build 25 reconciliation.
7. Both players and NPCs participate under equivalent applicable rules.
8. Completing a service grants appropriate experience.
9. Materials, money, ownership, obligations, and payments must be conserved.
10. Services should create opportunities for repeat work, employment, reputation, and eventual business development.

Use ordinary NPC judgment to determine willingness, prices, and whether a character accepts a request.

Do not guarantee customers, sales, or income.

DECISION 3 — FARMING YIELD AND FOOD SECURITY

APPROVED: Simulation-driven agricultural balancing, not an arbitrary fixed yield selection.

Do not assume that one proposed wheat yield is correct simply because it produces no starvation in a particular test.

Evaluate reasonable candidate yields using representative deterministic simulations, including integrated shared farming, seasonal farming, and the Living Economy.

Measure:

- Agricultural production and consumption.
- Household food access.
- Storage and distribution.
- Food prices and affordability.
- Livestock feed supply and affordability.
- Seasonal shortages.
- Trade flows.
- Starvation and economic hardship.
- Resource conservation.
- Differences across seeds and settlement conditions.

The objective is a sustainable but challenging agricultural baseline.

Most settlements should have a plausible opportunity to survive ordinary early winters, but prosperity is not guaranteed.

Poor decisions, genuine shortages, poverty, adverse circumstances, and economic failures must remain possible.

Distinguish actual food scarcity from failures caused by distribution, access, affordability, or defective economic behavior.

Do not solve a transportation or purchasing defect by artificially increasing crop yields.

Select and implement a defensible provisional baseline using simulation evidence. Retain measurement data for future balancing.

Farming skill improvements must preserve the existing distinction between faster work and crop yield.

Proceed without another owner approval of arbitrary yield numbers unless a new material design conflict emerges.

DECISION 4 — INDIVIDUAL CRIME-REPORTING JUDGMENT

APPROVED: Judgment-based witness reporting.

Witnesses and victims make individual decisions about reporting criminal activity.

Their decisions should consider:

- Crime severity.
- Actual harm.
- Relationships and family loyalty.
- Fear of retaliation.
- Personal disposition.
- Available knowledge.
- Individual judgment and circumstances.

A witness may report a stranger but protect a relative who commits a comparable offense.

Severe crimes should generally exert greater pressure toward reporting, but reporting is not universally mandatory.

Different witnesses may reach different conclusions.

Requirements:

1. Integrate the existing judgment-weighted reporting hook.
2. Preserve the distinction between witnessing an event and deciding to report it.
3. Never give NPCs information they could not legitimately possess.
4. Preserve individual variation and deterministic behavior.
5. Validate reporting interactions with guards, crime records, gossip, relationships, and consequences.
6. Activate the approved reporting behavior after its applicable validation passes.

No further approval is required merely to enable this approved feature.

DECISION 5 — FULLY INTEGRATED MATERIALS AND PRODUCTION ECONOMY

APPROVED: Expanded resources, production chains, and economic uses.

The economy should resemble the interconnected production philosophy of EVE Online, while avoiding excessive grind.

The world must support meaningful resource acquisition, processing, manufacturing, trade, and consumption.

Include the approved expanded material direction:

- Steel.
- Tin.
- Bronze.
- Silk.
- Fur.
- Deer antlers.
- Boar tusks.
- Other necessary materials supported by the approved production and equipment systems.

Mandatory rule: Every material introduced must have a legitimate acquisition or production pathway available to players and NPCs.

Examples:

- Steel is produced through appropriate metallurgy using actual inputs.
- Tin comes from real mineral deposits.
- Bronze requires appropriate constituent metals.
- Silk may initially be imported but must have a plausible local production pathway.
- Fur comes from appropriate animals and processing.
- Antlers can be obtained through legitimate hunting or natural shedding where modeled.
- Boar tusks come from actual boars.

Imported materials may be sold by merchants, but merchant inventory must not become the only possible source.

For off-map settlements, legitimate abstract production is acceptable where consistent with the regional simulation, inventories, and conservation accounting.

Do not generate unlimited merchant inventory or unexplained materials.

Materials should have useful applications beyond a single decorative recipe wherever reasonable.

Advanced equipment should require meaningful combinations of resources, expertise, and production infrastructure.

Do not impose arbitrary time-consuming grinds simply to create difficulty.

Integrate production with mining, hunting, agriculture, crafting, trade, employment, and settlement capabilities.

Preserve the game's resource sustainability and depletion rules.

DECISION 6 — ARMOR CLASSIFICATION AND SPECIALIZED BONUSES

APPROVED: Armor serves three distinct gameplay purposes while preserving the existing four catalogue classes.

A. Common and Fine Armor

These provide physical protection.

Material quality, construction, craftsmanship, and actual armor properties determine their defensive effectiveness.

Ordinary armor does not automatically provide magical enhancements.

B. Cultural and Regional Armor

These reflect specialized historical fighting traditions.

Examples:

- Samurai equipment may support sword handling, speed, or relevant melee techniques.
- Mongolian-style equipment may support mounted mobility, riding, or horseback combat.

Regional bonuses should reflect appropriate equipment functions and fighting techniques.

They must not depend on the wearer's ethnicity.

Anyone can acquire and use the equipment if they meet the actual applicable requirements.

Regional armor is not automatically superior to other equipment in every situation.

C. Rare and Elemental Equipment

Exceptional equipment can specialize in magical effects.

The owner reaffirms an elemental magic design beyond Fireball, including previously discussed fire, lightning, ice, wind, and other established elements.

Elemental equipment may enhance a particular elemental discipline, magical defense, or another specifically supported magical ability.

Requirements:

1. Preserve the existing four armor catalogue classes.
2. Distinguish physical, regional, and elemental bonuses.
3. Use equipment-specific bonuses rather than universal superiority.
4. Balance bonuses against actual gameplay mechanics.
5. Magical equipment complements a character's abilities rather than granting unlimited competence.
6. Rare crafting must consume legitimate resources and require appropriate expertise.
7. Reconcile the existing elemental-magic design records before implementing additional magic behavior.

Important scope boundary: This decision confirms the elemental equipment architecture and the broader magic design direction. It does not authorize inventing an unrelated complete magic engine or prematurely enabling unimplemented spells.

Where existing spells support an elemental bonus, integrate and test that bonus.

Where a required spell or mechanic is not yet implemented, identify the dependency honestly. Do not present an inactive bonus as functioning gameplay.

The broader elemental design must remain recorded for its appropriate implementation phase.

Numerical armor and bonus values are subject to evidence-based balancing.

DECISION 7 — ORDINARY CLOTHING IS COSMETIC

APPROVED: Clothing and functional equipment remain distinct.

Ordinary hats, bandanas, clothing, and decorative outfits provide appearance and personal style.

They do not automatically provide armor ratings or special bonuses.

A normal straw hat is clothing.

An enchanted sorcerer's hood is magical equipment.

The distinction depends on the item's actual function, not merely the material from which it is made.

Preserve ordinary clothing as cosmetic equipment.

Do not invent bonuses for ordinary garments.

Specialized magical headgear remains permissible under Decision 6.

DECISION 8 — PREDEFINED CAPABILITIES AND ORGANIC SOCIETAL DEVELOPMENT

APPROVED: A predefined library of possible capabilities with organic individual, settlement, and regional progression.

The game must know in advance which technologies, skills, techniques, recipes, and capabilities can exist.

The simulation does not invent entirely new technologies at runtime.

However, individual characters and settlements develop access to those predefined capabilities organically.

Individual Development

People learn through relevant experiences.

A person who mines stone may become proficient enough to perform prospecting or pursue more advanced mining activities.

Skills and activity-specific knowledge develop through legitimate learning opportunities.

Settlement Development

Villages maintain industry capability levels determined by their actual resources.

Those include:

- Residents and their skills.
- Equipment and tools.
- Infrastructure and buildings.
- Production facilities.
- Available resources.
- Relevant discoveries.
- Appropriate operational capacity.

Settlement levels must be derived from actual world conditions, not granted arbitrarily.

A village does not become an advanced mining settlement simply because one person reaches a particular personal level.

However, that person's expertise may contribute to establishing the necessary collective capability.

The current mining progression already defines surface extraction, prospecting, open pits, and underground mining.

Preserve and extend this existing architecture rather than replacing it with another progression system.

Regional Development

Settlement specialization affects trade and economic opportunities.

A settlement with developed mining capabilities may become a natural supplier for another settlement needing coal or metal.

However, other settlements must learn of that capability through legitimate knowledge, observation, commerce, or communication.

No omniscient global matching.

Progression Notifications

Provide clear but unobtrusive announcements for:

1. Personal skill advancement.
2. Newly achieved village industry milestones.

Example:

"Mining Level 3 Reached."

"Village Development: Mining Capability Level 2 Reached."

Record milestone history separately from current operational capability.

Current capability may decline if people, facilities, or resources are lost.

Do not repeatedly announce the same historical achievement during minor fluctuations.

Existing Implementation

Audit and reuse:

- "game/data/skills.json"
- "game/data/capabilities.json"
- "game/data/settlement_industries.json"
- Settlement experience and productivity systems.
- Settlement industry profiles.
- Mining capability adapters.
- Regional market and trade systems.

Do not double-count skill productivity through settlement-wide bonuses.

Settlement capabilities represent real capacity, not automatic extra output or currency creation.
