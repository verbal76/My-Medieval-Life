# Magic research register

Two parts, never mixed:

- **Part 1 (CANON / CANON-INFERRED)**: what *The Well at the World's End* actually shows. All 119
  magic facts are in `research/registries/facts.json` (`category: "magic"`), each with a paragraph
  citation and verbatim quote. The examples below cite chapters; the quotes are in the registry.
- **Part 2 (GAME EXPANSION)**: the owner's direction that magic in *My Medieval Life* is a
  learnable science, and how it can sit beside Morris's material. **Nothing in Part 2 is Morris's,
  and nothing in it is final** (decisions J-M1 to J-M4).

## Part 1. The supernatural in the novel

### 1.1 How the novel presents it

Morris almost never explains his marvels. They are told in three registers. Keep them apart:

| Register | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrated** | the narrator says it happened | the Sage makes signs and the pursuers see only stones and old bones (BTHREE-C7); a crow dies at once on drinking from the Dry Tree's pool (BTHREE-C18); scars vanish after drinking of the Well (BTHREE-C22); Burgers flee in terror on seeing Ralph (BFOUR-C29) |
| **Claimed by a character** | someone in the story says it | the Lady learned foreseeing and dream-sending (BTWO-C7); Bull says wild things love and obey him (BFOUR-C5); the Sage foretells (BTHREE-C8, BFOUR-C6) |
| **Rumour or belief** | hearsay, superstition, slander | the Dry Tree Queen shifts between fair and foul (BONE-C12); immortal youths under Goldburg's towers (BTWO-C33); the old Queen's ghost walks (BFOUR-C19); the Lady tried for sorcery (BTWO-C7) |

### 1.2 Phenomena by kind

| Kind | What the text shows | Register | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Well's water** | sweet water that brings a sweet sleep; heals old scars; long youth and life "through generations but not for ever"; strength; a commanding presence ("like the gods"); some say persuasion and luck in love | narrated (healing, youth), claimed (the rest) | the Well at the Ocean Sea's edge |
| **Foreseeing (spaedom)** | foretelling events and fates; reading palms | claimed, often borne out by the plot | the Lady, the Teacher of Lore, the wise carline, Martha, the Sage, Katherine, the Wise Woman of Sarras |
| **Sendings and dreams** | sending a dream or vision to another; a dead woman's "image" appearing in a dream | claimed (the Lady says she sent the friar's dream); narrated dream | the Lady, the vision of Ursula |
| **Semblance and illusion** | changing someone's appearance; making a camp look like stones and bones; changing a man's skin | narrated (the Sage, BTHREE-C7); claimed (the Teacher, BTWO-C7; Redhead about the Sage, BTWO-C38) | the Teacher of Lore, the Sage |
| **Beast-craft** | wild creatures love and obey; whispering to a horse to lead its rider astray | claimed | the Lady, the Sage, Bull Shockhead |
| **Ritual witchcraft** | a loom that weaves itself during a storm; goat sacrifice on a stone table; blood marks; seven faggots; singing from a great book; fear that opening the books will loose spells | claimed (the Lady's account of her childhood) | the witch-mistress |
| **Talismans and tokens** | the seekers' beads (lead a man, not a woman, to the Well); way-tokens on rocks marking safe paths and water; swearing by the beads or the Water | claimed, with way-tokens narrated | the Wise Woman of Sarras, the Innocent Folk, seekers |

| **Places of power** | the Dry Tree and its poison pool (beguiling death); the House of the Sorceress (one grows no older there, "a foretaste of the Well"); the Sea of Molten Rocks as the Well's "dyke" | narrated and claimed | |
| **Persons of uncertain nature** | the Lady (perhaps "blood of the fays"); the Queen of Goldburg ("of the fairy"); the Dry Tree Queen | rumour and speculation | |

### 1.3 Facts that bear on a "learnable" system

- **Morris's wise women learn and teach their craft.** The Lady says she learned foreseeing,
  dream-sending "and other things called sorcery" from the Teacher of Lore (BTWO-C7). The wise
  carline teaches her letters and lore (BTWO-C4). The Wise Woman of Sarras teaches Katherine
  wisdom (BFOUR-C28). There are books of lore: the witch's three great books, the book of the Well
  at Abundance, the Sage's heirloom book, and the book at the House of the Sorceress.
  *This is CANON precedent for teaching and learning lore. It is not CANON precedent for magic as
  a science with formulas.*
- **The Lady denies that some of her craft is sorcery**: her woodcraft is "no sorcery: she was
  bred in woods" (BTWO-C2). Morris lets skill and the uncanny blur.
- **Effort and cost**: the witch says something must be offered "lest all come to naught"
  (BTWO-C3). The Sage has not dared use the Well's gift "for fear of abusing it" (BTHREE-C9). The
  Innocent Folk fear that drinkers would become tyrant "earthly gods" (BTHREE-C16).
- **Limits**: the Lady "cannot foresee everything" (BTWO-C9). The Well gives long life but not
  eternal life (BTHREE-C16). The dead cannot drink (BTWO-C7).
- **Elemental spells do not appear.** No fireballs, ice or lightning in the novel. The closest
  things are illusion, foresight, sendings, beast-craft, healing water and fear.

## Part 2. GAME EXPANSION: magic as a learnable science

The owner has set this direction (Master Design Directive §8). The design below is a proposal
for discussion only.

### 2.1 Requirements (from the directive)

- Magic is learned like chemistry or metallurgy: formulas, methods, practice, experiment.
- Aptitude may speed learning but grants no exclusive right. No chosen-one power, no bloodline
  gate.
- Keep three things separate: **knowledge** of a formula, **proficiency** in executing it, and
  **capacity** to sustain its requirements.
- It coexists with every other profession; it has non-combat uses.
- NPCs and players obey the same rules. An NPC can teach what they understand.

### 2.2 How the science can sit beside Morris (options, not decisions)

| Option | Idea | Fit with canon | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-A **Lore is the science** | Morris's "lore" (foresight, sendings, semblance, beast-craft) are early, half-understood branches of the same discipline that the game formalises. Wise women and sages are practitioners. | Strong: matches CANON teaching and learning (§1.3) | The game must never claim Morris *meant* a science |
| M-B **Two traditions** | The game's elemental science is a newer school (GAME EXPANSION). Morris's lore is an older, intuitive craft that the science cannot fully reproduce. | Preserves Morris's mystery | Two systems to design |
| M-C **The Well is outside the system** | Whatever the science does, the Well's water is not reproducible: no formula gives long life. | Protects the novel's centre and keeps the Well rare | none significant |

**Recommendation**: M-A for lore-type effects plus M-C for the Well, with elemental effects
(fire, ice, lightning) as the game's own extensions of the discipline. Always label them GAME
EXPANSION in any in-game "lore" text that cites Morris.

### 2.3 What stays canon-protected

- Who has drunk of the Well: the Lady, the Teacher, the Sage, Ralph, Ursula, the woman of the book
  at Abundance (written, BONE-C19), and perhaps Goldburg's founder (rumour). Never extend this list
  as canon.
- The beads' rules, way-tokens, the Dry Tree's pool, the House of the Sorceress, the Sea of Molten
  Rocks, the Rock of the Fighting Man. Use them as written. Mark any game mechanics attached to
  them as expansion.
- The novel's ambiguity: who is or is not "of the fairy", whether the Dry Tree Queen shape-shifts.
  These stay UNRESOLVED rather than being settled by game systems.

### 2.4 Open questions for the owner (also in J)

- J-M1 Energy or capacity source (stamina, a reservoir, components, time). Not chosen.
- J-M2 Whether Morris-style lore (foresight, sendings, semblance) is learnable by players, and to
  what degree.
- J-M3 Whether the Well can be reached in play at all, and what drinking does mechanically.
- J-M4 Social standing of practitioners: the novel shows suspicion and trials for sorcery (the
  Lady, BTWO-C7; Agatha's protective rumour, BFOUR-C6). Should practitioners face legal or
  religious risk in some regions?
