# D. Political and faction atlas

Hand-written synthesis of the registries. Every canon statement below comes from a record in
`research/registries/` (facts, relations, spatial), where it is cited to the paragraph with a
verbatim quote. Chapter references here (e.g. BONE-C14) point to where the evidence sits. Look
the subject up in the encyclopedia (A) or gazetteer (B) for the quotes.

Labels: **CANON** = stated in the novel. **INF** = CANON-INFERRED. **GAME EXPANSION** = our
proposal, never Morris. **UNRESOLVED** = see J.

## 1. The shape of the problem

- **Power changes during the novel, sharply.** At the start the Burg of the Four Friths is the
  strongest power near the Wood Perilous, Gandolf rules Utterbol by terror, and the Abbot of Higham
  keeps the peace around Higham. By the homecoming the Burg has fallen and its people are exiled
  raiders, Bull Shockhead rules Utterbol, and the Abbot is a prisoner of the Westland Barons. In
  the epilogue Ralph is King of Upmeads, lord of the Scaur, captain of the Burg and of the
  Shepherds. Any game world has to pick an **era** (decision J-D1). This atlas records all three.
- **There is no map of kingdoms.** Morris gives towns, lordships, fellowships and wildernesses
  with a few directions between them, and almost no borders. Most of the world between the named
  places is open.
- **Most authority is personal and local**: a kinglet, an abbot, a tyrant, a town's Porte, a
  fellowship's captain. Nothing in the text describes an overarching kingdom or empire over the
  western lands (CANON by absence; recorded as a gap, not a fact).

## 2. Eras

| Era | Anchor in the novel | State of the west | State of the east |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 Before Ralph | BONE-C1 | Burg strong and at war with the Dry Tree and the Wheat-wearers. Abbot of Higham mighty; Upmeads at peace. | Gandolf tyrant of Utterbol. Queen of Goldburg. Tyrant of Cheaping Knowe. |
| E2 Homecoming | BFOUR-C17 to C31 | Burg fallen to the Dry Tree and Wheat-wearers; mixed new folk under a Dry Tree captain. Expelled Burgers raid Higham-land and the Downs and march on Upmeads. Abbot captive at the Red Mount; Higham-land half ruined. | Bull Shockhead lord of Utterbol, at peace with Goldburg (King Rainald). King of Cheaping Knowe as cruel as ever. |
| E3 Epilogue | BFOUR-C32 | Ralph King of Upmeads; holds the Scaur, captain of the Burg and the Shepherds; clears the Wood Perilous and the Wood Debateable; restores the Frank of Higham; frees three towns beyond the Wood Debateable. | Bull "warder of the peace of the Uttermost lands". |

The novel's own time-span between E1 and E2 is short: the Dame of Upmeads speaks of nearly two
years without news (BFOUR-C26). It is not precise; see J.

## 3. Regions and powers

Order follows Ralph's road: from home in the west outward, then east to the World's End. Region
names in *italics* are our working labels (GAME EXPANSION), not Morris's.

### 3.1 *The Upmeads country*

| Power | Kind | Ruler by era | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upmeads | petty kingdom of hills, meadows and yeomen's halls; no city, castle or abbey | E1–E2 King Peter, "a kinglet", with no freedom to tax and tail his folk. E3 King Ralph and Queen Ursula. | BONE-C1, BFOUR-C26, BFOUR-C31 |

| (unnamed) three good towns beyond the Wood Debateable | towns | E3 freed by Ralph from an oppressor | BFOUR-C32 |

Internal order: vavassors, yeomen, franklins and knights; a muster under Nicholas Longshanks at the
High House. Kingship passes by Peter's choice of "the meetest of the kindred"; the new king swears
in St. Laurence's choir and the vassals then swear to him (BFOUR-C31). **Gap:** the size of
Upmeads, its villages and its lords other than the royal house are not named; this is room for
original content.

### 3.8 *Beyond the Great Mountains*

No polities except the **Innocent Folk**: a peaceful people with a town nine days from the Vale of
Sweet Chestnuts. They guide seekers and fear that those who drink of the Well would become
"earthly gods" and tyrannise the rest (BTHREE-C13–C16). The House of the Sorceress, the Thirsty
Desert, the Dry Tree and the Well have no rulers.

### 3.9 Far away

- **Sarras the Holy**: an ancient city "in the eastern parts of the earth", reached by an exceedingly
  long chapman's journey; home of the Wise Woman (BFOUR-C28). Unplaced.
- **Kingdom of the king's son**: the Lady's early backstory, beyond heathen simple folk beyond the
  mountains (BTWO-C6). It may or may not be the Kingdom of the Tower (UNRESOLVED).

## 5. Gaps that original content may fill

1. Everything between named places: villages, manors, minor lords and roads. **This is where the
   starting county goes** (G).
2. Overlords: who, if anyone, stands above the kinglets, abbots and town Portes. Nothing in the text
   says.
3. The Wheat-wearers' homeland, the Westland Barons' lands and the Red Mount, the bishop-baron's
   lands, the three towns beyond the Wood Debateable, and Sarras: named but unplaced or unnamed.
4. Laws and taxes beyond the few stated (weapon licences, safe-conducts, tolls, let-passes, the
   king's oath, blood-money).
5. Population sizes. The only counts are armies: 554 and 1,083 for Ralph's host, "two thousand"
   Burgers, about a thousand riders for Gandolf.
