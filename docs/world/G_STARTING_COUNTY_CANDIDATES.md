# G. Starting county: candidate insertion regions

**Status: proposal for owner decision. Nothing here is final** (decision J-D2). The county and
everything in it is **GAME EXPANSION** (geographic certainty **Original**). This document only
asks where it can sit without contradicting the novel.

## Method

A candidate region must:

1. Contradict no spatial record (`research/registries/spatial.json`): no fixed direction,
   distance, journey time or travel sequence may be broken.
2. Not displace a named polity's land or a recorded event (the Burgers' march, the Battle of
   Upmeads, the Westlanders' war on Higham, and so on).
3. Connect to the literary world by roads or rivers that already exist in the text, so that
   leaving the county leads somewhere canonical.
4. Support an ordinary life (farming, crafts, trade, service) and allow but not force adventure.

Two textual facts open the space:

- **Upmeads' east and west are undescribed.** The Four-want-way, four furlongs from the High House,
  sends Hugh north, Gregory west and Blaise east (BONE-C1); only the north (Wood Debateable,
  bishop-baron) and south (ridge, higher hills, Wulstead, the Downs) are described.
- **The text gives distances between places, not borders.** Forty miles Wulstead to Higham
  (BONE-C3), "some twenty miles" Higham to the Wry Bridge (BFOUR-C21), thirty miles of highway
  east of Whitwall (BTWO-C16) and three days from Whitwall to the mountains (BTWO-C19) all leave
  room for original land beside the routes.

## Candidates

### C1. West of Upmeads, along Gregory's road (the Upmeads Water country)

- **Where**: west of the Four-want-way, upstream on Upmeads Water (it flows west to east,
  BONE-C1-P009), between the Wood Debateable to the north and the southern higher hills.
- **Canon anchors**: Upmeads Water (fixed course west→east), the Four-want-way's western road
  (fixed), the Wood Debateable's northern marches (fixed), and the Red Bridge and "the Uplands" by
  which the Burgers reached the wood (constrained, BFOUR-C23).
- **Constraints to respect**: Wulstead lies south of Upmeads and outside it. The Wood Debateable
  lies north. Upmeads stays a small kingdom of no city, castle or abbey, so a county there must not
  be *inside* Upmeads unless it is a lordship under King Peter (decision J-D3).
- **Fits**: a peaceful, farming-first start at the edge of the novel's opening region; the river
  carries trade down to Upmeads. Neighbours are a friendly kinglet, Wulstead's market, and the Wood
  Debateable's outlaws.
- **Era sensitivity**: high. In E2 the Burgers and the wood's strong-thieves march on Upmeads past
  this area (BFOUR-C23, C29): a ready-made regional crisis the player can ignore or join.
- **Risks**: very close to Ralph's home and story. It must be clear the player is a neighbour, not
  the hero. Gregory's road west is canonically someone's journey, but its destination is never
  given.

### C2. East of Upmeads, along Blaise's road toward Whitwall

- **Where**: the long country between the Four-want-way's eastern road and Whitwall. Blaise rode
  "up and down and round about" before reaching Whitwall (BTWO-C12), and Clement's company went
  from Wulstead to Whitwall through the Wood Perilous (BTWO-C14).
- **Canon anchors**: Whitwall (fixed), the Swelling Flood ford (fixed), "a captured rich town" on
  Blaise's way (BTWO-C12, an implicit, unplaced record), Swevenham four miles off.
- **Constraints**: no stated distance from Upmeads to Whitwall. The route via the Wood Perilous
  means the wood lies between them on that road.
- **Fits**: trade-route county; the player can head west to Upmeads or east to Whitwall's guilds.
- **Risks**: the least textual evidence, so the most freedom but the weakest "sense of place"
  from Morris. The "captured rich town" hints at war on that road.

### C3. The Down-foot vale between Wulstead and Higham, west of the Greenway

- **Where**: the thorp belt at the foot of the Downs toward Higham (Garton, Mileham, Milton,
  Netherton; BFOUR-C21–C22, BONE-C4), extended west away from the Greenway.
- **Canon anchors**: the Downs ("big downs, waterless, roadless", BONE-C3), the Greenway (south from
  Bear Castle, fixed), Bear Castle and its four-fire beacon, Saint Ann's Chapel, Higham
  (fixed, about forty miles from Wulstead), and the Westland Barons to the west of Higham.
- **Constraints**: Higham-land is the Abbot's to the east and west of the town (bale-fires of his
  vassals, BONE-C5). The Westland Barons' war reaches about twenty miles west of Higham. The
  Burger strongholds (Foxworth Castle, the Longford, the Nineways Garth) are "hereabouts", unplaced.
- **Fits**: a sheep-and-thorp economy (wool of the downs, shepherds' Folkmote), a frontier between
  church land and baronial war, and an armed shepherd kindred to join.
- **Era sensitivity**: very high. E2 is a war zone (Burger raids, the Abbot captive, Higham's lands
  half ruined). E1 and E3 are peaceful.
- **Risks**: dense with E2 events, so the county must coexist with the Burger strongholds.

### C4. The peopled country west of the Wood Perilous (Higham–Bourton side)

- **Where**: the "peopled parts" that the western road from the crossroads leads back to
  (BONE-C10), south and south-west of Higham's vine country, near Bourton Abbas.
- **Canon anchors**: the vine country south of Higham (little hills and dales with vineyards,
  orchards and cornfields, BONE-C7), Bourton Abbas (the Abbey's outermost land), the dry waste, and
  the Wood Perilous with its crossroads.
- **Constraints**: Bourton is reached by noon from Higham. The Burg is "more than an afternoon's
  ride" from Bourton. St. Mary's lies north of the Wood Perilous.
- **Fits**: a rich agricultural county (vines, orchards, corn) on the edge of the Wood Perilous:
  safe fields, a dangerous wood, the Burg, Hampton and Abundance within reach.
- **Risks**: Bourton Abbas holds of the Abbey, so the county must be a neighbour of Higham, not part
  of it, or be an abbey holding (decision J-D3). The Burg–Dry Tree war is close in E1.

### C5. The plain west of the mountains, near Whitwall

- **Where**: the dry highway plain of meads and acres between Whitwall and the mountain wall
  (BFOUR-C12), or south toward the "good town" fifty miles south of Whitwall (BTWO-C14).
- **Canon anchors**: Whitwall (fixed), the highway thirty miles east (fixed), the three-day road to
  the mountains (fixed), Swevenham and its sickness legend, and the Castle of the Waste March sixty
  miles from Swevenham.
- **Fits**: a guild-and-market start ("the last land" of courtesy and guilds, BTWO-C19), the
  gateway to the eastern adventure, and Swevenham's Well lore close by.
- **Risks**: far from Upmeads, so the novel's opening region becomes distant. Earl Walter's war in
  E1.

### Rejected for a starting county

- **Goldburg, Utterbol and the tyrant lands, and the lands beyond the Great Mountains**: canonically
  thinly peopled, tyrant-ruled or wilderness (BTWO-C19, C33). They suit later travel, not an
  ordinary-life start.
- **Inside the Plain of Abundance or the Burg's Frank**: fully described, small and owned.

## Recommendation (for discussion, not a decision)

**C1 (west of Upmeads)** if the owner wants the player to begin in the same "small quiet land" that
opens the novel, with the larger world revealed in the order Morris reveals it. **C4** if a richer,
more dangerous mixed economy is wanted from day one. Both depend on choosing the era first (J-D1),
because E2 turns either into a war zone.

Whichever is chosen, the county's internal geography (villages, manor, mill, roads, wilderness)
is fully original and generated under the constraints in H. Its edges join canonical routes:
Upmeads Water and the Four-want-way for C1; the crossroads road and Bourton's valley for C4.
