summary: Ralph mopes in Whitwall for days. On the sixth day Richard the Red shows him the new Guildhall, the Great Church (Mass at St. Nicholas's altar) and Blaise's booth, then takes him out by Petergate Bar into the many-watered meadows. There Richard tells him of the Well: he was born at Swevenham, a little town across the river about four miles off, where fifty years ago three young folk set out for the Well, taught by an old man of the mountains; the priest Sir Cyprian refused to bless them. Richard later served at the Castle of the Waste March, sixty miles from Swevenham. He will go to Saturday market to find Swevenham folk and bring back tales. Blaise has been offered the freedom of the city and a place among the Faring-Knights.
geo: Whitwall's east gate is Petergate Bar; outside are meadows, a land of many waters with streams and little bridges. Looking out: a wide plain with a river, little hills beyond the water, then blue mountains with snow on their tops in July. Swevenham's great grey church tower stands on the first little hill beyond the river, about four miles from Whitwall. The Castle of the Waste March is sixty miles from Swevenham. The old man of the Well-lore lived in the mountains some way off.
