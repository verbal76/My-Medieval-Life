summary: After a night in the foothills and four days through well-tilled but poorly housed rolling land, the company reaches Goldburg. It is built on three hills (buttresses of a long ridge that turns a wide river), on the ridge itself, and on both flat riverbanks, all girt by a great white wall that crosses the river as a bridge; the castle rises above the lower bridge. The vaulted gate-hall leads over the bridge-hall into the Water-Street, lined with quays and palatial arcaded houses of white, red and grey stone, with river barges whose masts lower for the bridges. Many folk are ragged and beg; others are rich; scantily dressed women recall the Wheat-wearers, and rich men buy house-servants. A woman, the Queen, rules Goldburg. The hostel in the market-place has a garden court and a marble fountain, but its furnishings are ordinary.
geo: From the mountains' feet, four days through a country of little hills and hollows to Goldburg. Goldburg straddles a wide river turned by a long ridge with three buttress hills; a great white wall surrounds it and bridges the river, very high on the plain side. The lower bridge carries the castle. The market-place lies a little back from the river in an angle of the ridge and a buttress. On the hillsides are poor post-and-pan houses, apart from the castle and the Queen's palace.
