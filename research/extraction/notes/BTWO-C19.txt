summary: Clement explains the road. Fewer than a score of armed men risk death or capture on the mountain ways to Cheaping Knowe. Whitwall is the last land of courtesy, manor custom, lawful lords, guilds and laws of Lineage and Port. Beyond, the folk are nominally Christian but keep heathen customs (oaths on devils' altars, horse-flesh at high feasts, spell-raising, blood on the doom-rings, even human sacrifice). Towns and castles are held by self-made tyrants with hired free men; tillers and workers are saleable thralls; free men must serve some tyrant. The lands are rich in wine, oil, wheat, cattle, sheep, mined metals and gems, but poor in crafts, so good for merchants. Clement hopes Ralph will turn back. Itinerary: three days to the mountains' entry, two nights in the open, then the Mid-Mountain House, a truce hostel where foes give each other an hour's grace, then a day and a night in the mountains to Cheaping Knowe.
