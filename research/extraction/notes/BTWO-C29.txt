summary: Clement will turn back in about twenty days and begs Ralph to return; Ralph refuses if the damsel is not found, citing his knightly oath (knighted last year by Duke Osmond in St. Laurence of Upmeads). Merchants know nothing of her; the Lord of Utterbol has not been in Goldburg for three months, and his name silences men. A merchant advises seeing the good Queen of Goldburg. The Queen, young, fair and wise, in a marble-pillared, painted palace set in gardens, hears Ralph's story and visibly loves him. In three days she gives him a letter for the Lord of Utterbol, speeds him toward Utterness (the last known town, near Utterbol and on a way to the Well), offers him refuge whatever happens, and confesses her love. Her kinsman of old founded Goldburg and was slain in a quarrel.
geo: Utterness is no great way from Utterbol and is the last town Goldburg knows of; a way to the Well runs through Utterness and by Utterbol. Hunters come to Goldburg from the mountain feet and the forests of the plain; mariners frequent it.
