summary: The Sage leads them into the rock-sea up a black scree into a grassy isle, and shows the carved token (a sword crossed by a three-leaved bough) that marks the only safe way, going and returning. They wind a few crow-flight miles a day past screes, streams and great fallen blocks, with hares, conies, heath-fowl and red foxes. On the first night the sky glows with an earth-fire. On the third day the lava becomes clinkered and jagged. From a high rock they see far to the north a pillar of fire on a ness, the Candle of the Giants, not far from Utterbol, whose kindling is said to bode ill to the lord in the red hall of Utterbol. On the fourth day near sunset the lava ends: a wide grassy plain with low rises and copses, grazed by unowned herds and haunted by bears, lynxes and lions, stretches to the great ness. They rest a day. The Sage offers to take them home; they refuse. He says he has not dared use the Well's gift for fear of abusing it.
geo: Entry into the rock-sea: a bight closed by a scree of black stones; beyond, a grassy isle narrowing east with glossy rock walls; then a winding way between lava walls with screes, little streams, and great fallen blocks. Then a maze of clinker lava. From a high rock, the Candle of the Giants is seen far north on a ness of the mountain wall. The rock-sea takes about four days to cross by the token route. East of it: a wide grassy plain with low rises, bushes and small trees, before the mountain ness. The lava once flowed between the mountains and the western hills.
