summary: Leaving the Sage's house (Ralph walking so the Sage can ride), Ralph and Ursula have a lovers' misunderstanding and reconcile. The Sage leads them through rugged high hills and deep valleys full of wild beasts (lions heard), along streams and twice over mountain necks, to a grassy valley with sweet chestnuts at dusk. He has them build a big fire against lions and says he will deal with the Utterbol riders. At night he makes signs, covers the lovers with his cloak and stands between the horses. Twenty-odd riders under the Lord's nephew and Otter arrive, but see only two grey stones, a skeleton leaning on the tree and old horse bones; the frightened nephew flees. Otter, lingering, says he is glad, would rather ride with Ralph than serve "the Devil and his Nephew", and follows.
