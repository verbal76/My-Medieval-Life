summary: Ralph comes down from the downs at sunset to Higham-on-the-Way on St. John's Eve. A monk leads him to St. Mary's Abbey guest-hall, shows him the Midsummer plays (St. George and the worm), the bale-fire and the hilltop bales of the Abbot's vassals, and urges him to serve the Abbot. The man in the sallet whispers "The second time!" in the crowd. Ralph sleeps in the abbey and dreams of a dried-up Upmeads Water.
geo: Higham lies below the end of the downs, overlooked by a white castle on a knoll, lapped by a river through green meadows. Three parish churches and the great Abbey church; a large paved market-square with the minster on one side; a gate. From the minster gallery bale-fires show to the west along the down headlands and in thorps by the river, and more to the east: the Abbot's vassals hold land on both sides.
