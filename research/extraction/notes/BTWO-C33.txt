summary: The Lord's train (Lord in a litter, forty horsemen, magnates, armed servants, a rear score) takes the Utterness road with Ralph unarmed among them. David says things beyond the mountains can kill a man by fear; that the founder of Goldburg drank of the Well with help from a learned woman, being one all women loved; and that he himself does not believe a man could reach the Well in a lifetime. At noon the sandy-haired captain explains that the Lord keeps the moorland between Utterbol and Goldburg poor so it cannot feed a hostile host; its few folk pay him tribute and some are his hounds, Morfinn the richest of them, whom the Lord unmanned. The Lord can raise a thousand men by sending the war-arrow; Goldburgers fight poorly outside their walls. Folk say that when Goldburg falls the world will change, and that under each Goldburg tower lies a youth and maiden who drank the Water and can die only by point and edge.
geo: The road to Utterness crosses rough, untilled, little-grazed moorland. The distant Wall of the World seems no nearer.
open: Is the woman who helped Goldburg's founder the Lady of Abundance? (David's tale echoes the Lady)
