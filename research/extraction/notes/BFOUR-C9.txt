summary: Redhead hires twelve men-at-arms for Ralph to Cheaping Knowe and advises a gift for its close-fisted, cruel King; Redhead then leaves. They cross the Goldburg mountains unhindered, the wild men having lately been crushed by Goldburg and the chapmen. They pass Bull Nosy's high howe, now topped by a standing stone carved with a bull and a sword. After a night at Whiteness they reach Cheaping Knowe on the third day. Through streets of maimed, hanged and whipped folk Ralph brings his gift to the King, a tall, fierce, black-bearded warrior among the chiefs of the Porte. Ralph says he has drunk of the Well; the hall falls back in awe and the King grows wheedling. Ralph asks only the freedom of the prisoners being led away, which is granted. That night Michael-a-dale of Swevenham, of the King's Council, comes in disguise to warn of an ambush three leagues out and offers to guide them by a by-road if taken home. The Sage vouches for him as kin of Michael of the Hatch of Swevenham. Michael says the King knows of Ursula.
geo: Goldburg to the mountains to Whiteness (one night) to Cheaping Knowe (third day). The ambush is planned three leagues from Cheaping Knowe toward the mountains; a by-road makes a detour of about twelve miles.
