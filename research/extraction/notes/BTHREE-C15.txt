summary: They leave with the Innocent Folk's messengers, who marvel at the horses (the elder once led a mounted knight to the Well in his youth). After three days they leave the mountains; in three more the mountains are only a cloud behind; then fair valleys and little hills without people. On the eighth day from the Vale they meet ruder shepherds with odd sheep, and on the ninth reach a wide, well-watered valley with cots, corn, cattle and vineyards. That evening they reach the Innocent Folk's town: simple, unwalled, low houses, no filth, famine or poverty, merry folk in white wool. At the Temple or Mote-house or Guest-house the elders hail them as "Seekers after length of days"; they are bathed, robed in white, crowned with flowers, seated on the dais like gods, feasted, and sung to.
geo: From the Vale of Sweet Chestnuts: three days out of the mountains; three more until they are a cloud behind; fair valleys and little hills, unpeopled; on the eighth day, a green hillside with shepherds; on the ninth, a wide grassy, watered, partly wooded valley with cots, cornfields and hillside vineyards; then the town of the Innocent Folk (unwalled).
