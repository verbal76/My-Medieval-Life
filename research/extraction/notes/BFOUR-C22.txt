summary: After midnight Giles comes to Ralph: an old shepherd of the Downs whom Ralph met on the Greenway (the Thirly drinking). The shepherds have been harried by the Burg-devils, their sheep lifted, some men slain and women taken. Tokens and an old tale of a helper from afar have roused them to arms; Giles repeats a rhyme of the Dry Tree and the Well-spring. The spaewife Martha, who lives alone near Saint Ann's Chapel, sent him to find a young green-coated captain at Mileham, Milton or Garton. Her message: do not turn aside to the Burgers at Foxworth Castle, the Longford or the Nineways Garth; other Burgers are marching from the Wood Debateable on Upmeads. She foresees a mote at Bear Castle and friends at Wulstead. Ralph resolves to ride to Bear Castle, light the four fires, hold the mote, go on to Wulstead and Clement Chapman, then home to Upmeads. Giles kisses Ursula, as is the custom of the Downs.
geo: Saint Ann's Chapel is in the Downs. Mileham, Milton and Garton are thorps at the feet of the downs toward Higham. The Burgers hold strongholds hereabouts: Foxworth Castle, the Longford and the Nineways Garth. Another band is marching from the Wood Debateable toward Upmeads. The route is Bear Castle, then Wulstead, then over the hills into Upmeads.
