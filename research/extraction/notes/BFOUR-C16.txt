summary: Next day they reach the Water of the Oak by late afternoon; Ralph refuses to camp there. They sleep by a westward stream and, guided by the Sage, reach the Plain of Abundance two hours after noon, unchanged. An old carle asks if Ursula is the Lady; he says she may be her sister but is not. Ralph tells them the Lady is dead, slain by the baron of the golden sun on blue whom Ralph killed, and that the baron's mighty brother is also dead. The folk ask Ralph to be their lord; he accepts in her memory, asking only that they keep the castle and give guesting, and promising to come if they send to "Ralph of Upmeads, down by the water". The Prior of the Thorn has said no one of the Lady's line, nor anyone but the Baron of Sunway, could claim the castle. They feast under an oak; the folk tell of wood-wights, wodehouses, elf-women and dwarfs, and a hermit north-west in the wood; the wood is a wall to them. The Fathers of the Thorn would carry any message.
geo: From the cave, through the wood to the Water of the Oak by late afternoon; a westward stream for the night; next day two hours after noon to the Plain of Abundance (ford, cots, white church, castle on its mound). The House of the Thorn is not far away. A hermit lives in a clearing a little north-west of the plain. The plain folk know only the plain, the wood's edges and the Thorn.
