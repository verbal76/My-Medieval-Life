summary: At nightfall they reach the stream cleaving the pine wood, high-banked, deep and strong; they hear a lion in the night. Next day they climb along it into rough shaggy upland, the mountain wall seeming no nearer than from Vale Turris; the stream sometimes runs between sheer cliffs. Ralph shoots heath-fowl. They camp on greensward between the water and a half-ring of steep bank, hear a distant roar of falling water, and see foam on the river. At dawn the Sage of Swevenham appears: white-bearded but strong, ruddy and unwrinkled, wearing beads like Ralph's. He leads them through beech wood to his clearing (about twelve acres, goat crofts, wheat, pot-herbs, a timber house with a carved rood, a spring) and says it is not the witch's house, which is far away but which they will reach. He lived near Swevenham, gave lore to seekers who never returned, and in old age sought and found the Well himself. He lists the way: the mountains and the Wall of the World, the Winter Valley, the Folk Innocent, the Cot on the Way, the Forest of Strange Things, and the Dry Tree. He has an ancestral book and dresses them in white linen albs with red girdles to hear it.
