summary: Hugh tells Ralph that Gregory is a novice monk at Higham and that Lancelot Long-tongue is a sergeant there. About a year and a half earlier the Abbot of Higham quarrelled with the Westland Barons and led his host against them. He was overthrown at the field of the Wry Bridge, some twenty miles west, and is held in their strongest castle, the Red Mount. The Westlanders failed to take Higham but took or burned most of the Abbot's castles, so Higham's lands are half ruined and its men are cooped up and churlish. The monks believe the Dry Tree is devil-aided, and they fear the expelled Burgers, who have taken strongholds from the Westland Barons. Hugh recounts his misfortunes: prison, a siege, an affair, living with outlaws at the wildwood's edge, then capture and pressed service with Higham. Ralph says Ursula is his wife and a Friend of the Well. At a little unwalled thorp under the first hill of the Down country, the elders welcome them; about a score of young carles volunteer. The riders lodge in a big barn on the western outskirt, facing the likely attack.
geo: The field of the Wry Bridge is about twenty miles west of Higham; the Red Mount lies further west. The thorp (later named as Garton) is under the hanging of the hill where the Down country begins on the road from Higham; it is unwalled; the attack side is the west.
