summary: They climb east up a long, stony, barren, nearly waterless mountain neck, camp at a rock pool, and next morning, after four hours, reach a rocky crest facing the Great Mountains: nesses topped with natural walls, pinnacles, giant clefts and eternal snow. Between lies a vast houseless, treeless grey plain, an ancient sea of molten rock. The Sage calls it the dyke and outwork guarding the land of the Well; no pursuit can follow into it. He charges them not to be tyrants or builders of cities like Goldburg's founder or the thrall-masters of the Burg, but to live in peace, help the oppressed, and love the earth. Ralph says he means to go home to Upmeads and keep it in peace; Ursula says she will help him. The Sage points to a ness with ruddy, recently burnt streaks: behind it is the Rock of the Fighting Man, the Gate of the Mountains. They must reach a certain cavern before winter. They descend to a green belt with waters between the ridge and the rock-sea cliffs, and camp in a grassy, rock-walled nook.
geo: East of the chestnut valley: a long stony, barren mountain neck; a rock pool; four more hours to a rocky ridge running across the neck, from which the Great Mountains fill the view. Between them: a vast grey plain of ancient lava (the rock-sea), "like a huge river or firth". A fire-scorched ness juts from the mountain wall like a bastion over the lava; behind it is the Rock of the Fighting Man. At the ridge's foot: a belt of greensward and waters before the lava, whose edge rises in cliffs up to about a hundred feet.
