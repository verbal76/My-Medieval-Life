summary: At Richard's house Ursula, dressed in Goldburg web, gives Ralph a garland she made. They ride a white palfrey and his horse across the meadow, over the bridge and through the orchard into the empty base-court, to the great hall doors. The hall is thirty fathoms long and full of Shepherds, Champions, Wulstead men and Upmeads folk. At the dais King Peter sets Ralph in the throne with Ursula beside him, and the folk hail the King of Upmeads. After supper the great cup called the River of Upmeads is brought to Peter. He proclaims Ralph King and Ursula Queen, and foresees Ralph bringing peace to other lands and towns. Within three days mass will be sung in St. Laurence and Ralph will swear the king's oaths, then the vassals will swear to him. Peter kneels and swears service at once. The old couple lead them to the solar, the kingly chamber, and bless them for offspring.
geo: From Richard's house the way runs across the meadow to the bridge-end, over the bridge, through the orchard to the base-court and the great hall doors; the hall's great door faces the bridge. The hall is thirty fathoms long, with a shot-window at the far end lit by the low sun, and a dais with thrones. The solar is the kingly chamber.
