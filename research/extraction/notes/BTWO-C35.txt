summary: At dawn Ralph sees the mountains in the east as a huge black wall, ranges rising one behind another, still far off. Otter muses that beyond may be a plain, endless mountains, or the world's edge over a howling gulf, and that the mountains make the local folk harsh, "thralls of the great mountains". He says "Wall of the World" may also mean something else no living man has seen; a much-worshipped Dry Tree stands not far from Utterbol on the hither side, said to be only an image of the true one near the Well; seekers from here never came back. Two hours before noon they reach the Dale of the Tower: a shallow dale with a river channelled into water-meadows by leats and hatches, a grange among elms, cots, and a great square tower on a mound flying the Lord's banner. The Lady of Utterbol's white, gold and purple pavilion is there. Otter says he was once a thrall and that in Utterbol the clever thralls rise above free men. The Lady is very tall with yellow hair and blue eyes; her women are barefoot thralls with iron arm-rings. When the Lord offers Ralph to her as a gift, she blushes and looks down at the sight of him.
