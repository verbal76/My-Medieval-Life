summary: Ralph rides east alone through the wood by the sun, glad to be free. He follows a woodland stream from the mountains until it turns too far north and sleeps by a pool in a wood-lawn. Next day the thick wood ends in hills and dales with harts and wild swine; he drives a bear from a honey tree and eats the comb, and eats grapes from wild vines on a south-facing slope. He finds the stream again, now running from the east, and sleeps by it; next day he follows it until it turns north, then goes east over hilly land of coarse grass, whin and thorn, seeing the black mountain wall, always climbing, until a great pine wood with no visible end north or south blocks the way late in the afternoon. He rides into it.
geo: The wood beyond the highway thins; a woodland stream flows from the mountains, its upper course from the east and its lower course turning north. After the thick wood: hills and dales, open, with deer, wild swine, a bear and wild vines on south slopes. Further east: patchy thickets, hilly land with long coarse grass, whin and thorns, rising steadily; then a great pine forest stretching north and south out of sight. The mountain wall is visible like a great black cloud.
