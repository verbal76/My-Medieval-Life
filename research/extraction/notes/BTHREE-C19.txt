summary: Past the valley of the Dry Tree the dead dwindle; the salt crust ends that day, the ridges lower over two days to a swelling plain with dwarf willow and harsh herbage. Fodder is gone and water nearly so. By night they camp by bushes and willow-herb; beasts roar. Next morning Ralph sees woodland on rising ground about two miles ahead and dark mountain tops beyond, with black rock reefs running east on either side (near on the right, far on the left), like those by the Fighting Man and rising as they go east. Three miles on, a winding, crystal-clear little river with black sands flows mostly east; a black rock by it bears the way-sign, so the water is safe. They drink, ford it knee-deep, bathe and rest. Ralph hears a far-off roar like many waters.
