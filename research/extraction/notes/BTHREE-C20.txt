summary: They rest in the wood, which is small; by sunset next day they are through it and before mountains that are rather great grass-grown hills, wooded at their feet. Entering by a waymarked pass, they camp in a dell on the biggest fell and hear the roar of the Ocean Sea, which girdles the earth. Next day, after five hours' climb, they see green slopes with trees, then three or four miles off the blue landless sea ahead and on both sides: they are near the end of a great ness. Below, the land simply ends at a cliff under a hundred feet high. On the point is a low green mound with a square stone carved with the token on every face, and a waymarked stair cut down the cliff toward the sea. They search a mile each way, find nothing else, and sleep behind the mound at the world's end.

A cliff under a hundred feet; a green mound with a carved square stone on the point; a stair cut into the cliff. The sun sets "beyond them" over the sea.
