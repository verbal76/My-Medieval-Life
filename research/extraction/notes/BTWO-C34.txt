summary: The Lord of Utterbol questions Ralph's skills and makes him sing, on pain of beating. Ralph sings a love song and then a battle song learned from Sir Karr Wood-neb, a Northland chieftain who wintered at Upmeads. The Lord prefers the love song but says his lady wife wants war songs and tales of fays, and plans to test Ralph in the tilt-yard. They travel on with Otter, captain of the guard: three hazy days, four rainy days climbing steadily, then on the ninth day of captivity long, low treeless downs with willow and alder hollows. Otter says they will reach the Dale of the Tower next day, where the Lord will hold a tourney to try Ralph. David says it is four days' ride from Bull-mead in the Dale of the Tower to Utterness, and twelve hours from Utterness to Utterbol, and that the mountains will be visible next day.
geo: After the moorland: pinewoods; three hazy days; four days of rain, generally uphill; then long, low treeless downs with stream hollows of willow and alder. The Dale of the Tower (Bull-mead) lies among the downs, four days' ride from Utterness; Utterbol is twelve hours from Utterness.
