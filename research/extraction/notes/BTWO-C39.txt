summary: The Lady despairs until Agatha says the Lord has agreed to her plot and her rumours are working. On departure morning the Lord, holding Joyce's hand, offers Ralph service at Utterbol; Ralph asks for the choice and the Lord sourly declares him free, with arms and horse, to ride with them or go. Otter warns that the freedom is a leash: from Goldburg to the mountains, and a hundred miles north and south of Utterbol, the Lord can seize him anywhere. Otter describes the great forest the road skirts to Utterness and beyond (Utterness is an island in a sea of woodland reaching to the Great Mountains), with wolves, bears and lions. The way to the Well runs that way; its pass through the Great Mountains is marked by a black rock carved with a Fighting Man or giant of old. A sage in the wood under the mountains can tell the way. Ralph secretly holds to Redhead's escape plan. The column leaves Vale Turris with wains and herds.
geo: The Lord's reach covers between Utterbol and Goldburg, between Utterbol and the mountains, and a hundred miles north and south of Utterbol. A great forest lies along the road to Utterness and beyond; Utterness is like an island in woodland that covers everything up to the feet of the Great Mountains and climbs them where the wall is broken; the road only skirts its edge. The pass through the Great Mountains is marked by a black rock carved with a Fighting Man. Vale Turris is the Latin name for the Dale of the Tower.
