summary: The search finds nothing. Descending, the wilderness improves; after noon they leave a deep pass cut by a swift stream (once much larger), climb a ridge, and see a great spreading plain with a bright break far off: Goldburg on a cluster of steep hills. Clement says Goldburg was built very fair by a lord who brought craftsmen from over the mountains, meaning to drink of the Well and live there in long joy; some say he drank, some deny it, and he was slain in a tumult before it was finished, the rest being cobbled up. His wish that all should thrive failed: Goldburg's toilers are mostly not thralls, but unsupported wage-workers without master or manor, worse off and starving, and easily replaced.
geo: Descending from the watershed: grass and bushes return; a deep pass by a swift stream that was once greater; a steep ridge across the road; below, a great spreading plain (rolling into downs and low hills on closer view) with Goldburg far off on a cluster of steep small hills, bright. From above Cheaping Knowe the mountains beyond Whiteness looked like a wall; here no such wall is seen.
