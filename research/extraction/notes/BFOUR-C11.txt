summary: Two days on they reach the Folk of the Fells' town in a hidden, defensible mountain valley: plain houses, the chief one like a great abbey barn where most people gather. The people are strong but not tall, black-haired, blue- or grey-eyed, cheerful and talkative; the women are comely and kind but allow no caresses from strangers. They keep no thralls: captives are sold, killed or ransomed, but captured children may be adopted with prayers to their gods, who are their forefathers. They feast the travellers three days and hunt hart, boar and wild bulls; Ralph excels and is thought a god. On the fourth day the chief escorts them by other ways, avoiding the Mid-Mountain House, down to the plain, where the Goldburg men take their wages and return with the wild men, who weep at parting.
geo: Two days' travel from the pass wood to a fair valley in the mountains holding the Folk of the Fells' town: hard to find and easily defended. From there, other ways (avoiding the Mid-Mountain House) lead down to the plain.
