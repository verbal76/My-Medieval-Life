summary: Epilogue. Ralph tells his whole tale to the Prior of St. Austin's at Wulstead while a learned monk writes it down, and this is supposed to be the source of the book. Ralph rules justly and delivers other lands and towns; his name alone halts hosts. He holds the Castle of the Scaur all his life and clears the Wood Perilous of thieves. The new folk of the Burg make him their lord and captain, and the Dry Tree obeys him while any last. He serves the Abbot of Higham as captain, drives out the oppressors and restores peace to the Frank of Higham. He is always captain and brother of the Shepherds. He clears the Wood Debateable, roots out the last Burgers and frees three good towns beyond the wood. He and Ursula visit the Land of Abundance yearly and revere the Lady's memory. About two years after the homecoming the Lord Bull of Utterbol comes with Otter and Redhead and rides to battle with Ralph. Thrice later Ralph, Ursula and Clement journey to Utterbol. Katherine visits often and lives long. Ralph and Ursula never seem to age; Ursula bears eight children and sees four generations. They die on one day and lie in one tomb in the choir of St. Laurence of Upmeads.
