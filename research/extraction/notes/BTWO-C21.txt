summary: Next day the road runs downhill with all waters flowing east; they camp at a round knoll in a valley with a view of the fruitful plain and Cheaping Knowe far off. In the morning scouts find the strong-thieves holding the pass below. The company (sixty-two, a score of men-at-arms) charges down a clay cutting six abreast, crying "St. Agnes for the Mercers!" The skin-clad wild men, with wide shields and weak bows, scatter; a foot battle follows on the hillside ("St. Christopher to aid!"), and Ralph, lightly speared above the knee, captures a shaggy blue-eyed hammer-man, Bull Shockhead, who agrees to serve him but not fight his own kin. By afternoon they reach green foothills with herds and homesteads and unfurl their guild banners.
geo: Beyond the Mid-Mountain House the road descends and all waters run east. A day's ride brings a valley with a round knoll overlooking the fruitful plain where Cheaping Knowe lies blue in the distance. About two hours on, a high bare ridge, then a cleft through a clay bank down into a valley (the thieves' pass), with a steep shaly cliff on its south side. Then a better road down to green unstony foothills with cattle, homesteads and fenced fields.
