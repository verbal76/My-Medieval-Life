summary: The company rides about a dozen miles and camps in meadows by the forest; Redhead, as a horse-boy, checks that Ralph will flee the next night. Next dawn the wains and herds go ahead. Redhead urges "Fail not to-night!" They ride about twenty miles along the forest edge and camp at sunset in a clearing. David leaves Ralph early, saying he will buy a horse. Redhead shows Ralph a dead old oak at the forest edge where he will wait with a fresh horse in two hours. He will not come himself, because he hopes to win the Lady's woman at Utterbol. He says the damsel is wise and wary, that whoever sees her loves her and does her will, and that those who have drunk of the Well are well regarded in this land; he will tell her of Ralph. Ralph waits, anxious for the damsel.
