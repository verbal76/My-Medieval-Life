summary: The company rides about fifty miles from Cheaping Knowe to Whiteness through fertile land worked entirely by thralls housed in hovels and long foul barracks, with castles and strong-houses scattered about. Clement says a fair house-thrall like the damsel would not be sent to the fields. At sunset on the third day they reach Whiteness: a long, straggling town of whitened, pargeted timber on a steep ridge at the end of a valley, under bare, terrible mountains, with low walls and no great castle; its lord pays tribute to Cheaping Knowe for peace and is a decent lord. The Deacon of the Chapmen says the Lord of Utterbol has not been in Whiteness for six months, and that the wild man brought the damsel to market but left suddenly without selling her, leading her alone toward the Goldburg pass, she riding a little horse with hands unbound.
geo: Whiteness is about fifty miles from Cheaping Knowe (three days' ride), close under the mountains: the door of the passes to Goldburg. The last day crosses confused hills before the great mountains, which are dark, bare and stony. Whiteness sits on a long ridge at the end of a valley, with nothing green behind it but bare mountains.
