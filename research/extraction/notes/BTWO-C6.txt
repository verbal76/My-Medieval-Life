summary: The Lady says she has drunk of the Well; the book was written by her foes, and the Well holds no evil but the Quenching of Sorrow and Clearing of the Eyes. She and the king's son go west through long deserts of wood and heath, perilous lands and a dreadful stony waste (crossed on the fourth attempt), and come out of the mountains in spring among a kind heathen folk who think them gods sent from beyond the mountains, and who ride horned cattle, having no horses. Leaving them, they reach richer lands with castles, abbeys and walled towns where the king's son is known. A champion abducts her and is slain; a felon baron drugs her lord and later becomes her enemy; a young knight helps and follows them. They reach the king's son's rich country, and the king asks if he has found the Well; he says yes and kisses her hand.
geo: West from the uttermost wilderness: deserts of wood and heath, evil lands, a dreadful stony waste, the mountains, then a heathen simple folk in woodland halls on a plain beyond the stony heaths; then richer peoples with castles, abbeys, churches and walled towns; the king's son's kingdom (his father's foes mostly on its far side), with a chief city and castle. The Lady says the road west from the witch's wilderness is the road from the Well, which she and Ralph will tread the other way.
