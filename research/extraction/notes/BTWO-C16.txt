summary: Richard reports what he heard at Swevenham. Old men disagree whether the Dry Tree (which Alexander saw) stands on the hither or further side of the mountains called the Wall of the World, near Goldburg, the last town on Clement's road. A woman is said to have given the Hampton champions their name. A carline names the three seekers of fifty years ago (Wat Miller, Simon Bowyer and Alice of Queenhough) and later seekers: the mountain wizard, Arnold Wright, two knights riding east, a woman with a gem chaplet and a sword on the highway thirty miles east of Whitwall, a knight who asked of the Lady with the beads, and five young men who left during a great sickness twenty years ago. A yellow-haired lad saw a small slender woman rider with a chaplet and an anlace in the same place five days ago, going "from unrest to the Well". Ralph believes she is the maiden of Bourton (Dorothea). He is reading a book of Alexander which mentions the Dry Tree and kings sitting in chairs in nearby mountains.
geo: Swevenham has a stone bridge. The wizard lived in the mountains a way off from Swevenham. A highway runs east from Whitwall; thirty miles east of the town it passes between stubble-fields. Goldburg is the last of the towns on Clement's road, no great way from the Dry Tree, on the hither side of the Wall of the World (one teller says the Tree is on the further side).
