summary: At morning low tide, half a mile of black sand with black rocks lies bare below the cliff. Built against the cliff foot is a pound of huge stones, seven feet high and forty feet square, filled by a spring from the cliff, overflowing and running seaward in a wide stream: the Well at the World's End. In the hallowed raiment they go down the stair to a ledge by the gushing water, where the Sword and Bough are carved with the inscription: "Ye who have come a long way to look upon me, drink of me, if ye deem that ye be strong enough in desire to bear length of days: or else drink not; but tell your friends and the kindreds of the earth how ye have seen a great marvel." Behind a stone door with a latten knob is a gold cup bearing the token and the posy "The strong of heart shall drink from me." Ralph fills it; Ursula drinks "to thy life, beloved", and Ralph drinks "to the Earth, and the World of Manfolk". The water is sweet, not salt. A sweet weariness takes them and they sleep.

A rock-hewn stair leads to a level ledge (bench-table) beside the gush. Light westerly airs.
