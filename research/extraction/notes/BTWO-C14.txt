summary: At Blaise's richly stocked booth, with the Upmeads sign of the fruitful tree by the water, Ralph meets Clement Chapman, who brings Dame Katherine's kiss. Clement goes south fifty miles, then east beyond the mountains in about eight days. In the Christopher alehouse Clement says he paid the Dry Tree's Bailiff toll for passing the Wood Perilous and heard they have lost their young Queen, which will make them bitter, the old Queen still living. The Dry Tree takes its name from the Dry Tree on the hither side of the Wall of the World; seekers misled by the name go to Hampton and are slain or join. Clement has seen the Wall of the World afar; his eastward road passes beyond two mountain ranges and three fair cities. He offers Ralph safe company that far.
geo: Clement came from Wulstead to Whitwall the nearest way, through the Woods Perilous. A good town lies fifty miles south of Whitwall. Clement's eastward route goes beyond the mountains seen from Whitwall and beyond further mountains, past three fair cities, toward the Wall of the World. The Dry Tree stands in the lands on the hither side of the Wall of the World.
