summary: The Innocent Folk, who call Ralph and Ursula "the Heavenly Folk", send them on with the elder and his two grandsons and five pack oxen. A day from the town begins a terrible rocky, stony, sandy waste with little foul water; Ralph marks the way for the return. After twelve days it mends; a day of marsh and scrub, then pines, a grass waste with game, then oak, chestnut and beech woods, entered near May. In full June they reach the House of the Sorceress, as on the arras, kept trim by Innocent Folk wardens by promise to the Lady. Ralph weeps for her. The elder's son and his wife are wardens; quest-care is hereditary in their family. Fifty years ago the elder saw the Sage of Swevenham go old and come back strong from the Well. The elder explains that his folk do not seek the Well: they live long and healthy without war or plague, the gods gave death so men would not weary of life, and a few made godlike by the Well might tyrannise the rest; but westerners rightly seek it. They show the Lady's sites and relics. For eight days Ralph and Ursula read the House's lore-book at the ancient altar, then set out alone with water-skins.
geo: From the town of the Innocent Folk: one day to a terrible waste (rocky, stony, sandy, some hills, little foul water), crossed in twelve days; then grass and sweet water; a day of marsh and scrubby thicket; pines; a well-watered, nearly treeless grass waste; then woods of oak and sweet chestnut with some beech (entered near May); the House of the Sorceress in full June. Near the House: the ancient altar, the snow-grave of the dead seeker, the place of the Lady's torment, the Vale (Dale) of Lore and the Heath above it.
