summary: At dawn Ralph secretly arms himself, crosses the Ford on his horse Falcon and rides south out of Upmeads to the cheaping-town of Wulstead. There his godmother Dame Katherine and her husband Clement Chapman feed him; she gives him a necklace with a gold box and money, and both speak of the Well at the World's End, said to lie beyond the Dry Tree. Clement describes the road south over the downs to Higham-on-the-Way, forty miles off.
geo: The Ford lies in the ingle of Upmeads Water by the High House. Riding south, Ralph leaves Upmeads by sunrise, among lands friendly to King Peter; Upmeads Water is then ten miles north. A high road runs north into Upmeads over a new bridge (King Peter and a house of Canons on the north side) and south to Wulstead. South of Wulstead: hill-country, the big downs, Bear Hill, waterless downs with shepherds' huts and hermit chapels; then the fertile plain with the little river Coldlake and Higham-on-the-Way, forty miles from Wulstead. Further: the Wood Perilous, lands beyond it, and afar the Dry Tree.
