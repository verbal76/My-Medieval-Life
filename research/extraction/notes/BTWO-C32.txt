summary: The land worsens to patches of tillage, dark heath, bog and scraggy woods. Twelve uncouth, savage armed men bow to the Utterbol seal and withdraw. Morfinn says Utterness and Utterbol folk are sure the Well exists and are the nearest people to it, that some have found it, and that a hermit sage in the woods near Utterbol can set seekers surely on the road. They lodge with a fearful woodman. Next day, on heath with islands of green pasture, Ralph sees a motionless cloud: Morfinn says it is the top of the Wall of the World, far from Utterness, and the Well lies beyond it. After a pine wood they find a pavilion bearing a black bear on a castle wall on gold. Ralph calls Morfinn a traitor and flees, but is caught and brought before the Lord of Utterbol: short, broad, dark-haired, sallow, with small bloodshot eyes and a harsh squeaking voice. With David the Sage, his scarlet-clad, spectacled chief counsellor, he appraises Ralph like a horse, pays Morfinn, and orders him to bring meek, unmarked women within three months. Ralph does not show the Queen's letter.
geo: Day 2 east of Goldburg: less fertile, scattered tillage, dark heath, bog, scraggy woods; woodmen's or hunters' houses by evening. Day 3: some miles on, heath with green pasture islands and cattle, nearly level. The Wall of the World is visible on the horizon as an unchanging cloud, not near Utterness. Then a long thick pine wood, and beyond it a well-grassed plain where the Lord camped by the road.
