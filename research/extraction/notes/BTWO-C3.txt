summary: The Lady tells her childhood: she does not know her birth or parents; she grew up in a thatched timber house in a forest garden with goats, under a richly dressed, stern woman she called mother. The wild creatures loved her. At about fifteen, in a storm, she saw a loom weaving by itself and the woman naked, singing from a great book. Next day the woman sacrificed the girl's white goat on a stone table in a ring of yews. That winter she found a dead woman in scarlet and fur in the snow; the mistress took from her a blue and green necklace like Ralph's, calling her another seeker who did not find the Well. Four armed men later visited, hoping for a guide to the Well.
geo: A forest house of timber and reed thatch with garden, crofts and goat garth; the great forest all around; a lawn walled by great yews with a stone table of four uprights and a plank, the only man-made thing in the wood besides the house. The Lady says she was bred in woods, though not these.
