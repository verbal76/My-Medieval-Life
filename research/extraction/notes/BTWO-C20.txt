summary: Two nights camping by thorps, then a third at the foot of a deep rock-walled pass (the mountains' entry). Next day up to the heaths and stony ways to a grassy stream valley. Next day a slow climb, cold at sunset, to a long stony ridge beneath a great snow-crested mountain where the Mid-Mountain House stands, long and low, of great stones. Below it Ralph finds a scrap of flower-embroidered green cloth like the Bourton maiden's gown. In the empty house the old warden and his wife say strong-thieves brought in a captive woman some days ago, tall, grey-eyed, brown-haired and sun-tanned, who had fought with a sword; she wore a torn green gown and, the wife tells Ralph privately, a necklace like his. Ralph says she is his sister; the wife suspects love and takes a lock of his hair as payment. Ralph resolves to free her.
geo: Day 1 and 2: tents by little thorps beside water. Day 3 evening: a hillside by the road at the entry of a deep pass between very high rock walls rising steep from the plain. Day 4: through the pass up to heaths, wild stony ways, a grassy valley with a stream. Day 5: climbing; a long stony ridge under a great spreading snowy mountain; the Mid-Mountain House, long and low, stone-walled and stone-roofed, with a garth.
